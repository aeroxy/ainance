What happened

Volatility eases, yields steady as risk returns

Risk appetite returned as volatility collapsed 22% to 19.98 and the S&P 500 rose 2.38% to 7552 with Nasdaq up 2.79% to 25264. Treasuries steadied with the 10-year up 1.1 basis points to 4.87% and the 2-year up 3.7 basis points, while the dollar index fell 0.61% to 101.68.

Fed commentary stayed careful on inflation after strong jobs, leaving investors to look ahead to CPI. Oil and bond yields moved opposite directions for a change, easing headline inflation fears and helping duration.

Sources Investor's Business Daily, Quantisnow, Bloomberg.com, CNBC, WSJ, Axios, AP News

Broad bounce led by chips, Nvidia falls on probe

US equities recovered last week's losses led by semis up 5.05% and tech up 3.73%, with AMD, Amazon and CrowdStrike among top movers on upgrades and AI demand headlines. The Dow rose 2.18% even as Nvidia slid 4.27% on 3.3x volume.

Nvidia's board probe into CEO Jensen Huang over alleged links dominated large-cap tape and pressured the Nasdaq 100 despite broad gains. First Solar and Monday.com fell on downgrades while many beaten-down software and chip names bounced, keeping stock picking over index shorts the better tactic.

Sources Eurasia Business News, Investor's Business Daily, Yahoo Finance, Schaeffer's Investment Research, Investing.com, Investing.com UK, StreetInsider, MarketBeat, ad-hoc-news.de

Oil down on diplomacy, gas and metals up

Crude fell to 10-week lows, WTI down 4.38% to 86.85 dollars and Brent down 5.77% to 92.65 dollars, on hopes for US-Iran calm after Trump called fresh strikes highly unlikely. OPEC output edged back toward pre-war levels, adding to the 1.28 billion barrel at-sea overhang narrative.

Natural gas jumped 7.10% to 3.21 dollars to a three-week high as colder forecasts triggered short covering and record LNG demand emboldened bulls, lifting prices above 3 dollars even with record output. Gold rose 1.87% to 4151 dollars and silver rose 2.72% to 53.35 dollars as the softer dollar and lower oil eased inflation fears, while copper rose 2.25% to 6.38 dollars on tight China supply.

Sources Financial Times, WSJ, GV Wire, Crude Oil Prices Today, Bloomberg.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, EnergyNow.com, Reuters, Kitco, Mining.com.au, Mining.com

Crypto lags equity bounce; Asia and Europe rebound

Crypto fell while equities rose, Bitcoin down 1.17% to 74863 dollars below its 50-week average and Ether down 4.39% to 2129 dollars with XRP down 5.14% to 1.09 dollars. Glassnode flagged deepening ETF outflows near 1.28 billion dollars and about 1 billion dollars in liquidations, with miners facing a 6.4% difficulty drop near 74000 dollars.

Asia rebounded behind the US lead, Nikkei up 2.33% to 58775 and Taiwan up 4.94% to 41661, while Hang Seng slipped 0.13% to 24448. Europe closed higher on banks and tech as oil slid, keeping global beta firm despite crypto and Nvidia-specific drags.

Sources Fortune, Yahoo Finance, 24/7 Wall St., FOREX.com, Benzinga, Bitbo, BeInCrypto, CNBC TV18, 朝日新聞, Anadolu Ajansı
