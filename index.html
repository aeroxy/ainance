What happened

Tariffs, Rates, and the AI Reversal

Monday's euphoria over Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO unraveled on Tuesday as the Nasdaq slid 1.51% to 23,726.95, the S&P 500 lost 0.53% to 7,198.81, and US semiconductors tumbled 7.10%. Citing people familiar with the matter, the Wall Street Journal reported that US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could unveil the findings of an investigation into foreign industrial subsidies and excess factory capacity as soon as this week, hitting China hardest while subjecting trade-pact partners including the European Union, Japan, and South Korea to levies of up to 15%.

Company-specific cracks deepened the retreat: Nvidia sank 5.86% with Kalshi pricing a 94% chance Huang testifies to Congress before January 2027, Anthropic fell below its $83 IPO price on disclosures that co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei each sold $280 million in the offering, Micron slid 10.97% on a BNP Paribas Exane memory-glut warning, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise plunged 16.31% on shrinking AI server margins, pulling Dell down 11.83%. Two-year Treasury yields rose 2.1 basis points to 4.687% and 10-year yields held at 4.924% after New York Fed President John Williams said inflation remains too high, keeping Kalshi odds of a 25-basis-point December hike at 92%.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, 24/7 Wall St., Reuters, Yahoo Finance, Quantisnow, www.tmgm.com, Investing.com, Kalshi, MarketWatch

Defensive Rotation, Retail Beats, and Biotech M&A

Underneath the hardware selloff, nine of the eleven US equity sectors advanced and the Dow rose 0.81% to 49,998.49, stopping less than two points shy of 50,000, while the Russell 2000 gained 0.48%. Communications led all sectors with a 3.47% jump, followed by health care at 2.92% after Sanofi agreed to acquire respiratory drugmaker Insmed for $41 billion, pharma's largest buyout in three years, lifting Sanofi's US shares 10.97% as Kymera Therapeutics surged 15.66% on eczema trial data despite pricing a $1.75 billion equity offering.

Consumer resilience also stood out ahead of Thanksgiving as November US consumer confidence climbed sharply. Dollar Tree soared 11.18% after beating third-quarter earnings estimates by 37% on trade-down shopper traffic, pulling rival Dollar General up 3.33%, while Dick's Sporting Goods rallied 14.59% on Foot Locker turnaround progress and Best Buy rose 2.70% after reporting 6.1% comparable sales growth, its fastest pace in five years. Medtronic bucked the healthcare advance, dropping 4.56% after quarterly revenue missed estimates following its diabetes spinoff.

Sources Fast Company, CNBC, Reuters, marketscreener.com, Kitco, MLQ.ai, Barron's, Clinical Trials Arena, The Pharma Letter

Asia's Chip Surge Meets Wall Street's Reversal

Asian semiconductor benchmarks spent Tuesday daytime celebrating Monday's Wall Street rebound, closing hours before US tariff reports and hardware earnings guidance turned sentiment upside down. Taiwan's Taiex surged 8.99% to 40,122.35 as TSMC jumped 9.85% near Taipei's daily limit, Japan's Nikkei 225 climbed 3.91%, and South Korea's KOSPI rallied more than 6% to close at 5,006.03, whereas Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.73% and India's Nifty 50 lost 1.75% following an IMF growth downgrade.

European markets caught the early tech fade, with the STOXX 600 slipping 0.66% as declines in ASML, Infineon, and miners outweighed Sanofi's rally. By the close of New York trading, US-listed TSMC ADRs had fallen 3.96% and TSMC's 24-hour perpetual contract on Binance had dropped 5.91% from Taipei's closing level, leaving Asian chip and server stocks exposed heading into Wednesday's open.

Sources Cointelegraph, 24/7 Wall St., TradingKey, 联合早报, Yeni Safak English, MLQ.ai, CNBC, Binance

Crypto Loses $60,000 as ETF Outflows Set Record

The crypto market declined 2.11% to $2.08 trillion as Bitcoin fell 2.47% to $59,768.01, giving back Monday's brief bounce to $64,505. US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled another $570 million led by BlackRock's IBIT, pushing November net outflows to a record $4.71 billion, while combined Bitcoin and Ether ETF redemptions hit $728 million and Kalshi traders priced just a 15% chance of Bitcoin reclaiming $63,000 by Friday evening. A 21Shares report attributed the slump since October's $6.4 billion Binance hack primarily to weak demand and forced liquidations, noting stablecoin supply contracted by $10.7 billion over 18 days.

Altcoins and crypto equities suffered steeper drops: Ether lost 2.95% to $1,675.78 after treasury company BTCS sold more than half its Ether holdings to repay $43.8 million in Aave debt, Solana sank 4.95% to $69.30, and Strategy fell 8.12% to $77.55 despite disclosing additional Bitcoin purchases. In Seoul, South Korea's Financial Services Commission unveiled a draft bill capping crypto exchange shareholder stakes at 20%, or 34% with regulatory approval, while restricting won stablecoin issuance to bank-majority groups, keeping XRP below $1 at $0.9197, down 2.02%.

Sources Cointelegraph, Yahoo Finance, The Block, Crypto News, CoinDesk, Kalshi

Hormuz Risks Lift Energy as Industrial Metals Slide

WTI crude rebounded from an intraday three-month low of $79.07 to gain 1.51% to $81.38, with Brent rising 1.57% to $90.77, after continued vessel attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and fresh US Iran sanctions unveiled by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent put shipping risk back into focus. US natural gas climbed 5.06% to $3.35 per MMBtu on strong LNG export terminal demand and New England pipeline restrictions, set against European gas prices up 163% over the past year.

Metals retreated sharply from Monday's brief bounce as tariff nerves returned: copper dropped 4.20% to $5.40 per pound and silver plunged 5.94% to $40.61. Although an early Tuesday quote still showed COMEX gold up 4.47% at $3,672.40, afternoon futures and 24-hour bullion perpetuals actually slid roughly 2.3% toward $3,557 per ounce as strong November consumer confidence and elevated yields kept bullion near 14-month lows following its steepest 60-day loss since 1982.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, Marine News Magazine, Reuters, Crude Oil Prices Today, USA Today, Kitco, Natural Gas Intelligence, Bloomberg.com, CNBC, Binance
