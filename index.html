What happened

US Equities Rebound on Chips, Though Nvidia Lags

Wall Street closed out its strongest week in more than a year during Friday's shortened post-Thanksgiving session, with the S&P 500 rising 1.60% to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq climbing 2.31% to 24,129.50, and the VIX dropping 11.16% to 23.81. Semiconductors led US sectors with a 4.17% surge and technology gained 2.80% as memory makers SanDisk and Micron extended their rebound and US Section 301 tariffs spared key chip markets, while Amazon climbed 3.96% on a reported record $7.1 billion in Thanksgiving online spending.

Dispersion remained sharp beneath the index gains. Nvidia closed up just 0.80% after paring early strength on a Blackwell report and slipped 0.46% after-hours ahead of CEO Jensen Huang's December 1 and 3 Congressional hearings, while Vertex Pharmaceuticals sank 5.24% on an FDA drug-review delay into 2027 and SpaceX jumped 4.83% even after its first earnings report showed a GAAP net loss.

Sources Eurasia Business News, Yahoo Finance, AP News, ABC News, ChartMill, 24/7 Wall St., digitimes, The Motley Fool, Stocks Down Under, Benzinga, CNBC

Beijing Stimulus Vow Lifts Asia and Europe, While Tariffs Test Trade

Equities across Asia and Europe advanced broadly on Friday, led by a 4.34% jump in South Korea's KOSPI, a 3.73% surge in Shanghai, and 2.11% gains in both the Hang Seng and Nikkei 225, while Europe's STOXX 600 rose 1.08% on tech and mining strength. Beijing's pledge of stronger market support and Chinese industrial profit growth reaching a five-month high fueled a 6.45% rally in Hang Seng Tech, even as survey commentary pointed to uneven factory momentum.

India's Nifty 50 was the lone regional decliner, falling 1.07% on profit-taking roughly three hours before reports emerged that an India-US trade deal is nearly done. Trade policy remained a broader flashpoint as proposed US tariffs of up to 40% on China and 15% on allies drew pushback from Republican lawmakers over Canada tensions and warnings from US soybean producers over export retaliation.

Sources Binance, News On AIR, Yahoo Finance Singapore, Business Standard, Yahoo Finance, WTVB, The Business Times, Dakota News Network, ABC News, https://economy.ac/, Asia Financial, Rediff.com

Yields Climb on December Fed Hike Odds, Yet Bullion Jumps

US Treasury yields snapped an eight-day bond rally as risk appetite returned and traders looked ahead to payroll data and persistent inflation pressures. The 10-year yield rose 8.8 basis points to 4.936%, the 2-year yield added 7.5 basis points to 4.710%, and the dollar index gained 0.36% to 104.04, while traders on Kalshi priced a 94% chance of a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December alongside 4% odds of a US recession this year.

Precious and industrial metals shook off higher yields and a firmer dollar, with silver jumping 7.92% to $43.23, copper rising 3.36% to $5.52, and gold climbing 3.23% to $3,650 on ETF dip-buying and Beijing's stimulus vow. Intraday futures trading showed the bulk of that metals rally occurred during Asian hours, with COMEX gold easing 0.69% and copper pulling back 1.61% after Hong Kong's close.

Sources Cointelegraph, WSJ, CNBC, Investing.com, FXStreet, BullionVault, Mining.com.au, Kalshi, Calculation

Oil and US Gas Slide Ahead of OPEC+ Talks

Brent crude fell 1.35% to $95.27 and WTI eased 0.42% to $83.33 ahead of the weekend OPEC+ meeting as traders weighed reports of a possible output increase, Iraqi talks for a higher production quota, and reported US pressure on Iraq and Venezuela to leave OPEC. Those supply-side headlines outweighed accusations of a deadly Iranian drone strike in Kuwait that prompted European calls for tougher pressure on Tehran.

US natural gas futures dropped 4.70% to $3.49 in the newly front-month January contract as mild weather forecasts curbed heating demand and LNG export bottlenecks kept a domestic supply glut trapped. Upstream consolidation continued regardless, with EQT agreeing to acquire Range Resources in a $13 billion all-stock deal.

Sources Reuters, The American Bazaar, Barchart.com, CBS News, EnergyNow.com, FXEmpire, marketscreener.com, Quantisnow, CNBC

Crypto Decouples Lower Under Heavy Spot ETF Outflows

Total crypto market capitalization slipped 1.17% to $2.08 trillion as Bitcoin fell 1.56% to $59,818 and Ether dropped 2.16% to $1,668, decoupling from the equity rebound. Bitcoin briefly topped $62,100 ahead of the Wall Street open before reversing below $59,000 as $228 million in leveraged positions were liquidated in an hour ahead of a roughly $7 billion options expiry, while November US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows reached $5.39 billion, Ether ETFs lost $523.3 million over eight straight sessions, and Kalshi odds of sub-$55,000 Bitcoin this year rose 8 points to 60%.

Under the surface, CryptoQuant reported Bitcoin's seven-day supply in profit-to-loss ratio fell to an 11-year low of 0.825 even as large holders with 1,000 to 10,000 BTC added roughly 121,000 BTC since late September. Elsewhere, Ethereum developers pushed Glamsterdam's Hoodi testnet rollout to December 15 after gas-pricing bugs, Solana gained 0.94% to $71.96, and Zcash rose 2.10% ahead of KuCoin's confirmed November 30 Monero delisting.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance, Crypto News, Crypto Briefing, Kalshi, Binance
