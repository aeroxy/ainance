What happened

Macro and rates: growth intact, AI capex fears dominate

US equities fell hard into the close on Fri 30 Oct with the S&P 500 down 1.82% to 7,580.07 and Nasdaq down 2.86% to 25,437.00, the worst day since June. The VIX rose 12.91% to 19.42 while the 10-year yield eased 5.6 basis points to 4.933%, as AI spending concerns were cited for offsetting otherwise strong US growth and credit-market warnings resurfaced.

Fed officials justified rate hikes amid persistent inflation and the stock of federal debt topping $40 trillion kept fiscal focus high into US midterms. History pieces noted AI names have lost about $5 trillion recently but also flagged the market's typically strong stretch after elections. Positioning into the vote stays cautious.

Sources Kalkine India, Seeking Alpha, صحيفة مال, ABC News, NewsNation, Investor's Business Daily, Yahoo Finance

US stocks: tech-led drop, defensives bid

Technology fell 4.89% to 179.51 and semis fell 6.98% to 522.49, the worst US sectors, while communications fell 1.29%. Microsoft and Nvidia both beat on earnings with Nvidia revenue reportedly up 106%, but shares still slid with the market. Reddit fell 29.22% to 91.73 on heavy volume after Q3 clashed with user outlook and Google traffic worries, and Sandisk fell 13.47% to 1,150.89 on muted pricing guidance despite record revenue.

Defensives stood out: energy rose 1.91% to 70.02, consumer staples rose 1.41% to 89.42 to a record high, and utilities, health care, materials and real estate also gained. Coverage pointed to a near-complete rotation into defensive and high-quality names as investors seek safety. Volume in staples and semis exceeded averages, underscoring the split.

Sources ABC News, Microsoft, Barchart.com, Yahoo Finance, Stocktwits, TIKR.com, TradingView, Moomoo

Asia split on China support talk

Asia split on Fri 30 Oct: Nikkei fell 2.12% to 63,290.11, KOSPI fell 3.48% to 5,730.74, Taiwan fell 2.49% to 45,211.83 and Shanghai fell 1.44% to 3,599.02, while Hang Seng rose 1.46% to 23,294.42 and Nifty rose 0.51% to 23,053.10. The yen firmed to 157.53 per dollar after the Bank of Japan held rates.

Gains in Hong Kong followed reports that China's banks and regulators will meet on measures to support the market and arrest the slide. The reports are still unconfirmed but were widely cited and coincided with Hang Seng outperformance. Korea's $1 trillion wipeout linked to AI worries and a weak TAIEX backdrop kept regional beta soft.

Sources Bloomberg.com, South China Morning Post, BBN Times, ChinaTalk, Taipei Times, The Business Times

Crypto: hack clean-up helps, ETF flows still soft

Crypto steadied after a turbulent week: bitcoin rose 0.38% to 78,046.68 and ether rose 0.49% to 2,267.29 in 24 hours, while Solana fell 1.56% to 94.66. Binance resumed withdrawals in phases starting with bitcoin, BNB and stablecoins after the $6.3 billion hack, which the FBI attributed to North Korea, with the firm saying it covered most losses.

Flows remain weak: bitcoin ETFs saw about $643 million in daily outflows and ether ETFs extended outflows to 23 straight days, an all-time high streak, with Solana also seeing persistent outflows. Tron rose 2.02% to 0.3136 to lead majors after Binance reopened Tron-network withdrawals. Headlines also flagged $2.5 billion in exchange outflows post-hack.

Sources CNN, WSJ, CoinDesk, TradingView, PBS, BBC, CryptoSlate, 24/7 Wall St., CryptoPotato, bloomingbit, CryptoRank, www.tokenpost.com

Commodities: oil firm, metals slide

Brent crude held at 113.43 and WTI at 104.12 with OPEC+ holding December output steady amid market uncertainty. Banks hiked oil price forecasts on prolonged Gulf disruptions and weekly outlooks noted Brent testing September highs above 100. US oil giants were reported to see profit surges on the Iran war backdrop.

Gold sat at 3,898.90 near a one-year low and silver at 49.28, with coverage warning both may extend losses into the US vote and jobs data after gold slipped 1.5% and silver crashed nearly 5%. Forecasts flagged downside to $45 for silver if the tech selloff deepens. US natural gas held at 2.90 with Waha hub prices turning positive for the first time since February as LNG export flows hit a one-month high, though record supply and El Nino risks cap upside.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Seeking Alpha, Daily Sabah, EnergyNow.com, StoneX, Business Standard, The Indian Awaaz, NDTV Profit, FOREX.com, Moneycontrol.com
