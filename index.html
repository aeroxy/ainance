What happened

Rates and Macro: Fed Hike Talk Pushes 10-Year Yield Past 5.15%

US Treasury yields climbed again on Friday after October wholesale prices rose 0.3% on the month, lifting the annual producer inflation rate to 6.5%, the highest in nearly four years. Even though the monthly print was softer than some pre-market forecasts, the elevated annual pace kept Federal Reserve officials on the offensive: San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said the central bank will likely need to raise interest rates amid worsening inflation, and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack backed gradual rate increases as price pressures broaden.

The hawkish commentary pushed the US 10-year yield up 5.5 basis points to 5.158% and the 2-year yield up 1.4 basis points to 4.878%, while the dollar index gained 0.23% to 103.41. Reports also highlighted growing pushback inside the central bank against Fed Chair Warsh's view that artificial intelligence productivity gains would tame inflation on their own.

Sources Bloomberg.com, Reuters, CNBC, The Washington Post, finchannel, Moomoo

US Equities: Chipmakers Tumble While Defensive Sectors Lead

Higher bond yields and lingering fallout from CEO Jensen Huang's resignation at Nvidia dragged US technology shares down 2.07% and semiconductors down 3.03%, pulling the Nasdaq lower by 0.94% to 25,126.58 and the S&P 500 down 0.44% to 7,559.35. Advanced Micro Devices tumbled 8.75% and Broadcom lost 4.76% after a reported analyst downgrade, reversing their prior-day sympathy gains, while Nebius sank 9.92% and Cisco shed 6.24% as its quarterly beat failed to calm long-term growth worries.

Beneath the tech selloff, capital rotated briskly into defensive and value pockets, leaving the Dow up 0.06% at 52,328.86. Consumer staples led US sectors with a 1.36% gain, followed by communication services up 1.03%, energy up 0.92%, financials up 0.75%, and both health care and utilities up 0.51%, while industry reports pointed to a $54 billion surge in defensive ETF flows.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Barron's, Barchart.com, marketscreener.com, InvestmentNews, Wealth Professional, Benzinga

Europe and Asia: STOXX 600 Sets Record as Hang Seng and STI Fall

European equities weathered the US technology slide, with the STOXX 600 edging up 0.02% to 664.14 for its third consecutive record close. Goldman Sachs lifted its 12-month target for the benchmark to 700, pointing to easing regional energy costs and resilient corporate earnings growth as investors diversify away from high-multiple US tech.

Asian markets finished mostly lower on Fed rate-hike bets and AI hardware uncertainty, though Taiwan rose 1.35% and Japan's Nikkei 225 held a 0.53% gain. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.55% on worries over tighter US AI regulation, South Korea's KOSPI surrendered an early chip bounce to close down 0.98%, and Singapore's Straits Times Index dropped 0.96% as Genting Singapore hit a one-year low on weaker quarterly earnings even as Singtel reported a 10% rise in underlying half-year profit.

Sources investingLive, MLQ.ai, The Business Times, BigGo Finance, IDNFinancials, Reuters, Moomoo

Crypto and Commodities: Bitcoin Breaks $70,000; IEA Sees Surplus

Rising real yields and a firmer dollar pressured non-yielding assets across crypto and metals. Bitcoin fell 3.56% to $68,906.76, breaking below $70,000 as cumulative year-to-date outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs topped $1.5 billion and Robinhood reported a 48% October drop in crypto trading volume, while Ether slid 3.40% to $1,948.58. Gold dipped 0.20% to $3,877.60 as Goldman Sachs lowered its year-end target to $4,150 per ounce, and silver dropped 2.63% to $48.75.

Energy markets diverged on shifting supply balances. WTI crude ticked up 0.12% to $88.85 and Brent gained 0.25% to $96.06 as ongoing Middle East disruption risks and the US blockade on Iran offset an International Energy Agency report showing global oil supply returning to surplus and a 100% jump in Russian diesel exports following US sanctions relief. US natural gas fell 3.11% to $3.24 on a widening storage surplus and strong El Nino winter forecasts.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Bitcoin Foundation, Bloomberg.com, WSJ, The Guardian, Kitco, EnergyNow.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, The Times of India
