What happened

Nvidia's Governance Test and the US Sell-Off

US stocks retreated across every sector on Tuesday, sending the S&P 500 down 1.06% to 7,338.05, the Dow down 1.68% to 50,743.66, the Nasdaq down 0.78% to 24,319.54, and the VIX up 12.18% to 25.70. Traders cut risk ahead of Anthropic's upcoming IPO and Nvidia's third-quarter report—its first under interim CEO Colette Kress since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned last week following reports of extramarital affairs, including one involving an alleged Chinese spy.

Options price a $335 billion market-value swing for Nvidia, which slipped 0.48% to $155.39 and sits down 27% from a month ago. Reports indicated the board ruled out reinstating Huang despite supportive remarks from President Donald Trump, while directors formed a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee and ARK sold $84.8 million in Nvidia shares. On Kalshi, traders price 97% odds, up 14 points, that Huang testifies before Congress before January—with an appearance set for December 1—and 22% odds, down 7 points, that Rene Haas becomes permanent CEO.

Beneath the index surface, Financials led declines at 1.91% and Utilities fell 1.59%, Home Depot lost 2.45% despite beating earnings estimates as climbing mortgage rates weighed on housing retail, and PepsiCo slid 3.01% after Bernstein cut it to Sell. AECOM bucked the downturn with an 11.08% jump on an upbeat 2027 profit outlook, and Nasdaq 100 futures edged higher after the closing bell.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, HousingWire, Reuters, Yahoo Finance, Kalshi, Barron's, CNBC

Hawkish Fed Pricing Meets a Pause in Yields

Firm US consumer data reinforced a hawkish Federal Reserve backdrop as October retail sales rose 0.2% overall and 0.6% at the core alongside inflation at a three-year high. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said the central bank likely needs several rate hikes by spring to rein in prices, echoing Chicago Fed warnings over sticky inflation. Kalshi traders price a 25-basis-point Fed rate hike in December at 90% and November CPI above 3.6% year-on-year at 82%.

Even so, equity volatility drew haven demand into sovereign bonds on Tuesday. The US 10-year Treasury yield edged down 1.4 basis points to 5.107%, the 2-year yield fell 3.4 basis points to 4.793%, and the dollar index held nearly unchanged, down 0.04% at 103.50.

Sources WSJ, CNBC, Reuters, ABC News, WTTW News, Kitco, Kalshi

Europe's Bank Shock and Asia's Chip Divide

Europe's STOXX 600 dropped 1.75% to 650.72 for its worst session in eight months, hit by tighter US restrictions on advanced AI chip sales to China and a 5.47% slide in US-listed Deutsche Bank shares after whistleblower leaks of internal documents highlighted past Epstein payments, a Trump tax leak, and Russia bets.

Asian chip hubs absorbed heavy selling as Bank of America's fund manager survey showed tech overweights slashed from 37% to 12%—Taiwan fell 2.31%, Japan's Nikkei lost 1.34%, Singapore's STI dropped 1.43%, and South Korea's KOSPI ended at 4,968.48, reported as its lowest close since February. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.03% after mainland factory output and retail sales missed estimates, splitting Chinese tech between a 5.21% drop in sanction-hit Alibaba and a 6.77% surge in domestic chipmaker SMIC. India's Nifty 50 rose 0.38% as a low-AI-beta haven, while Beijing and Ottawa cut agricultural and steel tariffs and Washington concluded the framework of a trade pact with Thailand.

Sources Cointelegraph, MLQ.ai, Free Malaysia Today, The Business Times, ASHARQ AL-AWSAT English, Bloomberg.com, Yahoo Finance, Moneycontrol.com, Moomoo, CNBC, Reuters

Crypto Pressured by Hack Fallout and Tech Rotation

Bitcoin fell 1.61% to $63,976.80 even after BlackRock's spot ETF snapped an 11-session, $2.35 billion outflow streak with $76.5 million in Monday inflows—recovering roughly 3% of prior withdrawals as sentiment hit extreme fear. CryptoQuant data showed short-term holder realized-loss ratios sank to a nine-month low of 0.955 with 48.5% of supply at a loss and open interest at a two-year low of $20.498 billion, while retail traders rotated capital toward Anthropic's IPO and Kalshi priced just 36% odds of Bitcoin reaching $65,000 by Friday.

Ether dropped 1.14% to $1,799.70 as developers delayed Glamsterdam testing over risks to 15,000 contracts, while wallets from the $6.4 billion Binance hack moved $137 million in Ether. Regulatory and cyber scrutiny deepened after Fed Governor Christopher Waller cited the Binance hack in warning over tokenized finance, four US senators questioned SEC oversight of tokenized stocks, Germany's Bundestag backed 30-day non-criminal crypto freezes, and Google reported hackers using autonomous AI agents to exploit software flaws. Solana bucked the slump, rising 2.38% to $73.09 after the Solana Foundation took a majority stake in CoinGecko.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, CoinDesk, Bitcoin Foundation, Decrypt, Kalshi

Energy Risk Premiums vs. Industrial and Precious Metals

Seaborne crude held a geopolitical risk premium as Brent rose 0.41% to $92.82 while WTI slipped 0.09% to $85.64, with ongoing Gulf tanker attacks and reported Iranian strikes offsetting recovering Middle East exports and prompting Citi to raise its 2026 Brent forecast. US natural gas climbed 2.11% to $3.19 off technical support on colder US weather forecasts and a rebound to 86 euros in European TTF gas.

Industrial and precious metals diverged along growth and rate lines: copper fell 0.58% to $5.97 on weak Chinese industrial data and hawkish Fed hike projections, while gold dipped 0.05% to $3,851.20 near one-year lows and silver gained 0.60% to $47.14 as moderating Treasury yields offered temporary relief.

Sources WSJ, Reuters, investingLive, Yahoo Finance, BullionVault, Natural Gas Intelligence, Crude Oil Prices Today, ASHARQ AL-AWSAT English, Barchart.com, EnergyNow.com
