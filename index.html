What happened

Macro and rates: midterm Treasury bid

US Treasuries rallied after Democrats' midterm win pushed yields lower, with foreign inflows pivoting to dollar bonds and talk of a lower yield path next. That eased pressure after a week where hot payrolls of 165k had left the 10-year near 4.86% and the 2-year near 4.51%, and the dollar near 102.3.

The move helped stocks steady: the S&P rose 0.3% to 7376.86 and the Nasdaq rose 1.1% to 24579.42 on Friday even as Fed hike odds sat near 76% and CPI loomed. With VIX still elevated near 25.6, the market sits in a fragile oversold bounce where lower yields can support defensives until inflation data lands.

Sources WSJ, Bloomberg.com, MarketForces Africa, BusinessToday Malaysia, www.tokenpost.com, Investor's Business Daily

US equities: semis-led bounce after rout

US equities closed a wild week higher into Friday: semis rose 3.6% to 463.96 after an 11.5% crash, tech rose 1.5% and the Russell rose 0.9% while the Dow fell 0.5%. Quantum names bounced off yearly lows after raised forecasts and materials earnings beat on revenue more than EPS.

The bounce remains contested, with bubble and crash warnings, an 895 billion AI financing debate, and stock-specific drags from dilution and insider sales. Oracle's solid results failed to lift the stock and CoreWeave eyes record lows into a 21% implied move, keeping two-way risk high for Monday's US session.

Sources Quantisnow, TradingView, 24/7 Wall St., Investor's Business Daily, Benzinga, StoneX

Asia: Japan leads down, Singapore banks wobble

Asia finished last week sharply lower: the Nikkei fell 6.0% to 57434.77 toward a bear market as Advantest and Tokyo Electron plunged while TOPIX rose, Taiwan fell 4.5% and the STI fell 2.2%. Hong Kong had just posted its best week since March before giving back 1.3% to 24481.86.

Singapore banks sold off after Q3 results at DBS, OCBC and UOB, and AI fears were flagged as a driver of the tailspin. That leaves defensives and cash-generative names better placed at Monday's open around 09:00 SGT than high-beta tech if US yields stay soft.

Sources BBN Times, scanx.trade, BusinessToday Malaysia, The Straits Times, The Business Times

Crypto: record outflows but tape holds

Crypto held flat on heavy outflows: Bitcoin rose 0.1% to 75750 and Ether fell 0.1% to 2227 despite weekly Bitcoin ETF outflows near 1.28 to 1.47 billion and Ether marking a sixth straight week of redemptions near 365 million. Headlines noted a 412 million daily Bitcoin outflow with 93 million from Ether, led by a single BlackRock fund, and talk of a fifth straight losing week for Bitcoin.

Price action showed resilience: Bitcoin held 75000, Solana edged higher on RWA and staking narratives, and BNB led majors with a small gain as the fear gauge eased after midterms. The tape suggests seller exhaustion near support, with the next leg hinging on whether daily flows turn.

Sources CryptoPotato, www.tokenpost.com, Benzinga, CryptoSlate, Startup Fortune, CryptoRank, 24/7 Wall St., AMBCrypto

Commodities: crude soft, gas and gold firm

Oil eased on diplomacy hopes even as physical tightness lingers: futures slid with Brent near 98.32 and WTI near 90.83, while a record 1.28 billion barrels sit at sea and a Qantas fuel-bill warning flagged demand strain. After a 17.5% slide, resistance near 92.53 caps rallies without confirmed supply relief.

Gas and power tightened in contrast: global LNG spike risk rose on low European stocks up 140% on a 14-year low, and Egypt deepened cuts to fertilizer makers. Gold held flat near 4074.90 as China bought 26 tonnes in October and Goldman flagged a rebound case, with silver near 51.94 facing a CPI test.

Sources WSJ, Yahoo Finance, investingLive, 24/7 Wall St., EnergyNow.com, The Cool Down, gCaptain, Brave New Coin, TheStreet, FXEmpire
