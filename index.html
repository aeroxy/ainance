What happened

AI Unwind and Semiconductor Breakdown

Wall Street's post-IPO AI unwind deepened into a broad equity correction on Friday: the S&P 500 fell 3.81% to 6,902.25 for a seventh straight daily loss, the Dow dropped 2.03% to 48,102.46, the Nasdaq sank 5.37% to 22,596.71, and VIX surged 40.58% to 39.98. Anthropic's $81.34 billion Nasdaq debut at $83 per share drew $60 million in net first-day retail buying—roughly half of SpaceX's June debut—and shares faded as low as $87.27 on Friday, sapping risk appetite across technology (-7.39%) and semiconductors (-10.80%).

Company-specific shocks accelerated the chip breakdown. Nvidia tumbled 13.80% to $125.84 after Reuters reported an SEC investigation into its accounting, knocking its market value below Alphabet's ($265.70, down 2.55%) for the first time in over two years as Kalshi traders priced 96% odds that Jensen Huang testifies before Congress before January 2027. Broadcom dropped 12.12% to $304.65 and Micron fell 13.86% to $594.73 on reports that Google cut Broadcom AI chip shipments and bypassed it for high-bandwidth memory, leaving Consumer Staples (+0.63%) as the only positive US sector while Health Care (-0.25%) held nearly flat on managed-care upgrades.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, 24/7 Wall St., Reuters, MarketBeat, Moomoo, Kalshi, Benzinga, Barron's, MarketWatch

Hawkish Fed Repricing Meets Haven Bond Buying

Equity turmoil drove a flight into sovereign bonds even as Federal Reserve officials leaned toward tightening: the US 10-year Treasury yield dropped 12.8 bps to 4.924% and the 2-year yield fell 15.8 bps to 4.621%, while the dollar index held nearly flat at 103.86 (down 0.03%) and haven yen demand pulled USD/JPY down 0.76% to 158.46.

Fed Governor Lisa Cook said rate hikes are needed to lower high inflation in remarks explaining her October dissent, while Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson argued for deliberate rate hikes and policymakers warned of inflation risks tied to AI investment, following Governor Christopher Waller's call earlier in the week for a 50 bps move. On Kalshi, traders price a 25 bps December rate hike at 90% across 2.3 million contracts, while the odds of the S&P 500 finishing 2026 in positive territory fell 13 points to 66%.

Sources Cointelegraph, Reuters, CNBC, WSJ, Bloomberg.com, Baltimore Sun, Kalshi

Asia Chip Contagion and Tariff Divergence

North Asian chip hubs bore the brunt of global selling even before Wall Street's deeper Friday semiconductor slide: Taiwan plunged 6.23% to 36,033.29 to cap its worst week since 1991 as TSMC fell 4.76% amid reports it is halting some China chip orders under a new US rule, while South Korea's KOSPI lost 5.21% and Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 5.06%. China's central bank kept lending rates unchanged for an 18th consecutive month as Shanghai slid 1.22% and the Hang Seng fell 1.89%, Singapore's STI dropped 2.05% below 5,000 amid US Treasury scrutiny over Iran sanctions compliance, and Europe's STOXX 600 fell 2.00% for its steepest weekly loss since March.

Bilateral US trade policy carved out a sharp exception in India, where the Nifty 50 rose 1.12% to 25,800.60—the lone gain among major global equity benchmarks—after Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal confirmed Indian goods will receive preferential US market access and lower tariffs. President Donald Trump also signed a trade deal with Thailand, whereas New Zealand exporters face a reported 13% US tariff hit coming out of APEC.

Sources MLQ.ai, 24/7 Wall St., 联合早报, Agenzia Nova, Reuters, BeInCrypto, B2B News, News On AIR, TradingView, The Globe and Mail

Record ETF Outflows and Security Shocks Hit Crypto

Crypto sold off alongside equities as institutional funds rotated out of spot products: Bitcoin fell 5.95% to $58,428.95 after US spot Bitcoin ETFs logged $582 million in Thursday net outflows, led by a single-day record $541 million exit from BlackRock's IBIT that brought November redemptions to $3.47 billion and pushed 2026 net flows to negative $3.11 billion. Ripple's CEO said investors sold XRP and Bitcoin to buy Anthropic shares and Cathie Wood's ARK trimmed Ethereum and Solana funds for $90 million of Anthropic, pulling XRP down 7.74% to $0.8878 and Solana down 7.70% to $67.46 as Kalshi odds of Bitcoin hitting $50,000 before $100,000 rose 9 points to 36%.

Security incidents and regulatory pressure deepened altcoin losses. Ether fell 9.06% to $1,601.38 and BNB sank 8.10% to $534.24 as wallets tied to Binance's October hack moved $594 million in stolen ETH via THORChain and Chainflip despite a $6.5 billion Abu Dhabi freeze order, while Sky Protocol saw over $2 billion in USDS withdrawals after disputed illicit-flow claims. Hyperliquid's HYPE slid 10.30% to $60.46 after Hong Kong's SFC put the venue on its Alert List, the CFTC ruled out special US exemptions, and wrapped Monero token XMR1 crashed 99.8% in a suspected $13.5 million exploit; the US Senate also passed the DEATH BETS Act barring violent-event prediction contracts.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, Yahoo Finance, Bitcoin Foundation, The Block, Crypto News, 24/7 Wall St., Kalshi, CoinDesk

Precious Metals Crack While Weather Lifts Natural Gas

Rate-hike expectations overpowered both equity panic and lower nominal yields across metals, sending gold down 4.42% to $3,522.70 and silver down 7.84% to $40.88—their lowest levels in over a year—while copper fell 3.19% to $5.45 as unwinding AI data-center bets wiped out its 2026 gains. Retail accounts on Binance's silver perpetual remain crowded long at a 6.38 ratio even as Kalshi traders cut the odds of silver ending November above $40.99 by 27 points to 55% in a thin 10k-contract market.

Energy commodities bucked the liquidation on weather and transit risks. US natural gas climbed 5.45% to $3.45 for a third straight weekly gain on colder US weather forecasts and tight South Central storage, outweighing high Canadian inventories, while WTI crude rose 1.06% to $84.09 and Brent added 1.14% to $93.03 as Houthi shipping strikes and skepticism toward a US-China-Russia APEC supply vow offset temporary US waivers on Russian oil sanctions.

Sources CNBC, The New York Times, Marine News Magazine, WSJ, Natural Gas Intelligence, IndexBox, Bloomberg.com, BigGo Finance, EnergyNow.com, Kalshi, Mining.com, Binance
