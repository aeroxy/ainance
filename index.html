What happened

Huang's Return Drives Chip Surge With Sharp Tech Dispersion

Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated founder Jensen Huang as president and CEO ahead of Monday's US open after consulting senior US officials, concluding his return posed no national security risk even as an independent foreign-intelligence probe continues and Huang remains scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1. Nvidia surged 31.48% to $165.05, propelling US semiconductors up 15.03%, the Nasdaq up 6.47% and the S&P 500 up 4.71% while driving the VIX down 24.63% to 27.05. Europe's STOXX 600 rose 1.81% for its strongest session in over six months as tech shares recovered.

Beneath the headline surge, single-stock dispersion and post-open reversals were wide. Server makers Dell and HP Enterprise jumped 20.46% and 17.38% ahead of earnings yet faded 11.03% and 5.65% from their opening highs, while Semtech tumbled 8.77% in extended trading to $95.00 after reporting results despite an 18.83% regular-session gain. CrowdStrike plunged 8.92% after agreeing to acquire Zscaler in a dilutive $43.9 billion all-stock swap, recent AI listing Anthropic slipped 1.43% amid options hedging, and UnitedHealth fell 1.11% on proposed Medicare Advantage star-rating rules reported to threaten $107 billion in sector payments.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, The New York Times, Yahoo Finance, Quantisnow, Reuters, Moomoo, Kalshi, Stock Titan, Binance, CNBC, Calculation

Asia Set for Tech Catch-Up Amid Beijing's Policy Floor

Most Asian markets closed Monday before Nvidia's board announcement hit the tape, leaving a wide gap into Tuesday's open. India's Nifty 50 dropped 1.75% and Singapore's STI slipped 0.43% during Asian hours, while Taipei's benchmark rose 2.16% aided by market-stabilization curbs before TSMC's US depositary receipts surged 14.08% in New York and its round-the-clock Binance perpetual climbed 7.38% past Taipei's close.

Policy support also cushioned Greater China and Southeast Asia. Reports said Beijing unveiled 400 billion yuan in stimulus, lowered margin requirements to 80% and stepped up state ETF purchases to a record 90 billion yuan, helping Shanghai edge up 0.15% and Hong Kong finish nearly flat, down 0.12%. Meanwhile, the European Union and Indonesia signed a pact eliminating most bilateral tariffs, lifting the US-listed Indonesia ETF 5.04% on Monday.

Sources Jakarta Globe, Rediff.com, 联合早报, 재경일보, Investing.com South Africa, Seeking Alpha, 파이낸셜뉴스, CNBC, Binance, Calculation

Yields Climb as Traders Price December Fed Hike Over Warnings

As equity volatility collapsed and safety bids unwound, the US two-year Treasury yield climbed 5.9 basis points to 4.672%, the ten-year yield edged up 1.0 basis point to 4.922% and the dollar index rose 0.52% to 104.34 ahead of Core PCE inflation data. On Kalshi, traders price a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December at 94% on heavy volume, aligning with Fed Governor Lisa Cook's warning that financial conditions remain too loose with October inflation estimated at 3.8%.

That market pricing persists despite growing pushback from policymakers and prominent investors. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned Sunday that the central bank risks jeopardizing the economy by overtightening, while hedge fund manager Bill Ackman argued in commentary that tariffs, defense outlays and AI infrastructure spending justify a 3% inflation goal rather than risking recession to restore 2%. Higher short-end yields kept defensive consumer staples down 0.09% and limited real estate to a 1.08% gain.

Sources Cointelegraph, CNBC, StoneX, Bloomberg.com, Crowdfund Insider, Kalshi

Metals Rebound From Washout as Supply and Weather Sink Energy

Liquidated metals bounced alongside equities after Friday's margin-driven flush. Gold rose 4.47% to $3,672.40, silver gained 4.57% to $42.95 and copper climbed 2.56% to $5.63 as reviving AI data-center sentiment and Chinese stimulus reports offset a firmer dollar. Open interest on Binance's gold and silver perpetuals fell 14.87% and 19.68% over 24 hours, pointing to short covering and cleaner positioning rather than fresh speculative leverage.

Energy moved sharply the other way, pulling the US energy sector down 1.12% as Monday's worst-performing group. WTI crude dropped 4.00% to a three-month low of $80.07 and Brent fell 3.65% to $89.19 as increased Middle East flows and a Libyan pipeline restart outweighed Persian Gulf attack headlines, with Kalshi traders pricing high odds of normal Strait of Hormuz vessel traffic. US Henry Hub natural gas tumbled 6.65% to $3.18 on warmer domestic weather forecasts and recovering production, decoupling from an 8.6% cold-snap jump in European gas because near-capacity US export terminals keep domestic weather in control.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Reuters, The Guardian, WSJ, BullionVault, Crude Oil Prices Today, Barchart.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, Mining.com, Kalshi, Binance, Calculation

Spot ETF Outflows and Debt Sales Cap Major Crypto Behind Solana

Major tokens failed to match Wall Street's risk-on surge after an early short squeeze past $64,500 faded, leaving Bitcoin up 0.62% at $61,293.84 and Ether up 0.35% at $1,727.30. Confirmed fund data showed US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $1.79 billion last week, their third-largest weekly outflow on record led by $1.37 billion out of BlackRock's IBIT, extending four-week withdrawals to $4.34 billion while Strategy disclosed spending $211.8 million on preferred-stock buybacks versus $29.6 million on Bitcoin. In Ether, an SEC filing confirmed BTCS sold roughly 27,300 ETH for $43.8 million to pay down Aave debt alongside an eight-week Ethereum ETF outflow streak.

Capital rotated selectively toward alternative networks and market infrastructure. Solana gained 3.90% to $72.91 as US spot Solana ETF inflows resumed and corporate holder DeFi Development extended its buying streak to 11 straight weeks, whereas XRP lagged at a 1.32% gain after November ETF outflows topped $30 million. Separately, OKX and NYSE parent ICE launched OKXICE for round-the-clock trading of tokenized US securities over Uniswap v4 pools despite objections from 67 issuers, while Britain's FCA barred crypto exchanges from market-making on their own venues.

Sources Cointelegraph, CoinDesk, Bitcoin Magazine, Bitcoin Foundation, Traders Union, TradingView, The Block, CoinGecko
