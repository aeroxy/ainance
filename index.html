What happened

Tariff Relief Lifts Global Tech While Mainland China Stalls

With US cash markets shut Thursday for Thanksgiving after a mixed Wednesday session — where the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% and the Nasdaq fell 0.62% while the Dow rose 0.33% and the Russell 2000 gained 0.71% — overseas equities rebounded as investors judged newly proposed White House tariffs less disruptive for US allies than feared. Taiwan's Taiex surged 2.50% on a broad semiconductor recovery, Hong Kong's Hang Seng climbed 1.19%, Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.86%, Singapore's Straits Times Index added 0.65%, and India's Nifty 50 gained 0.25% on foreign inflows into information technology shares.

Mainland China was the notable holdout, with the Shanghai Composite rising just 0.01% to 3,476.88 as Washington framed its proposed levies around Chinese industrial overcapacity and Beijing pushed back. Meanwhile, round-the-clock stock perpetuals on Binance pointed to a firmer Black Friday half-session for battered US semiconductors and mega-cap tech, pricing TSMC up 2.07%, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF up 1.87%, Nvidia up 1.71%, and Alphabet up 1.19% above Wednesday's regular closes.

Sources Barron's, Bloomberg.com, Gotrade, 24/7 Wall St., bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com, MLQ.ai, The Business Times, The Edge Singapore, The Washington Post, CBC, Finimize, Binance

ECB Succession Continuity and Tobacco Deal Talk Boost Europe

Europe's STOXX 600 advanced 1.75% to 636.98 as bank and semiconductor shares rebounded alongside easing US tariff worries. Sentiment in European financials also drew support from a Financial Times report that eurozone governments backed Bank for International Settlements chief Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde as European Central Bank president on February 7 after the Netherlands withdrew support for rival Klaas Knot, though the appointment awaits formal approval.

Single-stock action in London centered on Imperial Brands, which jumped 13.27% on more than four times its 20-day average volume — reported as its biggest daily gain since 1999 — after Reuters and Bloomberg reported that Altria and Japan Tobacco are exploring a roughly $30 billion cash takeover and carve-up of the cigarette maker. Because New York was closed Thursday, Altria shares have yet to reflect the potential balance-sheet strain of a cash bid.

Sources Cointelegraph, Reuters, Finimize, MLQ.ai, Barron's, Bloomberg.com, Investing.com, CNBC

Hot Inflation Anchors December Fed Hike Bets Ahead of Blackout

US Treasury yields steadied into the holiday break, with the 10-year yield down 3.8 basis points at 4.864% and the 2-year down 2.1 basis points at 4.650%, while the dollar index edged up 0.11% to 103.78. Even so, October's 3.8% PCE inflation reading kept rate-hike expectations firmly anchored: traders on Kalshi price a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate increase in December at 92% and zero rate cuts in 2026 at 98%.

Brookings Institution economist Robin Brooks argued in commentary that hiking rates while energy costs sit below recent highs risks driving real rates sharply higher ahead of the December 8–9 policy meeting, whose pre-meeting Fed communications blackout begins Saturday. Gold added 0.86% on thin holiday turnover to $3,566.20 and silver rose 2.94% to $41.23, though bullion remains down 17% from last Thanksgiving — its steepest Thanksgiving-to-Thanksgiving slide since 2013 — under the weight of elevated real yields.

Sources Cointelegraph, WSJ, Around Prague, BullionVault, FXStreet, Kalshi, CNBC

Hormuz Shipping Risks Lift Crude as Mild Forecasts Pressure Gas

Global oil prices climbed Thursday despite holiday-thinned futures volume, with Brent crude rising 2.03% to $96.53 and WTI advancing 1.47% to $84.91. Shipping incidents in the Strait of Hormuz, alongside Middle East refinery and mine disruptions, offset rising US crude inventories, while Reuters reported from OPEC+ sources that the group is likely to roll over its first-quarter production targets at its upcoming meeting as member capacity disputes persist. Kalshi traders assign just a 9% probability that average weekly Hormuz traffic tops 30 in 2026, reflecting expectations that transit bottlenecks will linger.

US natural gas moved the other way, with the January contract falling 1.61% to $3.60 as mild Thanksgiving weather eroded Northeast cash premiums and warm December forecasts blunted record liquefied natural gas export demand. Base metals barely budged in thin holiday trading, with copper up 0.22% at $5.35 after Wednesday's 2.07% rally in US materials shares.

Sources Reuters, EnergyNow.com, Baird Maritime, Yahoo Finance, ChemAnalyst, FXEmpire, Natural Gas Intelligence, Kalshi, CNBC

Bitcoin Holds $60,000 Amid Record ETF Outflows and DeFi Stress

Total crypto market capitalization rose 0.65% to $2.10 trillion as Bitcoin gained 0.81% to $60,766, Ether rose 0.87% to $1,704.77, Solana climbed 0.95% to $71.30, and XRP added 1.09% to $0.9526 ahead of Ripple's 1-billion-token escrow unlock on Monday. Beneath the calm surface, spot turnover across major coins dropped roughly 40% to 50% below recent weekday averages, and Farside Investors data showed US spot Bitcoin ETFs hit a monthly record $5.39 billion in November net outflows after shedding $453 million Wednesday, with only Morgan Stanley's and WisdomTree's funds recording monthly net inflows.

DeFi and privacy tokens saw sharper strain. Sky called an emergency vote to shift 25 million USDS after withdrawals left $3,270 in its $158.48 million stUSDS pool, while Zcash slid 4.98% to $721.02 after Zakura developers urged moving roughly $302 million from its Sapling pool over AI risks. Globally, the UK FCA reportedly cited October's $6.3 billion Binance hack to advance DeFi rules, Japan floated tighter DeFi oversight, Spain empowered Sepblac to freeze crypto accounts, and Senate Democrats urged the SEC to scrap tokenized-stock exemptions. Still, Fidelity filed for a Nasdaq spot Chainlink ETF and the ECB detailed June 2027 collateral plans for licensed DLT bonds.

Sources Cointelegraph, BigGo Finance, CoinMarketCap, CoinGecko, Calculation
