What happened

Macro and Rates: Hawkish Fed Pushes 10-Year Yields Past 5.1%

Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid said the central bank still has work to do to bring inflation down, while Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee warned stubborn price pressures could force more aggressive rate hikes. On Kalshi, traders price an 88% probability of a 25 basis point rate hike in December and an 11% chance of a larger move, while odds that November year-over-year CPI prints above 3.6% climbed 7 percentage points to 92%.

The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 3.6 basis points to 5.139% even as the 2-year yield dipped 1.2 basis points to 4.852%, lifting the dollar index 0.23% to 103.41 and pointing US equity futures lower. Higher US yields spilled into Asian bond markets ahead of key Japanese GDP, trade, and inflation releases, contrasting with China, where record People's Bank of China cash injections pushed domestic bond yields to historic lows.

Sources Investor's Business Daily, WSJ, Bloomberg.com, San Luis Obispo Tribune, Traders Union, News On Japan, Vocal, www.tokenpost.com, Kalshi, TradingView

US and European Equities: AI Derating Drives Defensive Rotation

US benchmarks split on Friday as the Nasdaq fell 0.91% to 25,136.27 and the S&P 500 slipped 0.40% to 7,561.67, while the Dow was the only major US index to gain, up 0.10% to 52,347.55 as the VIX rose 3.21% to 19.30. Semiconductors tumbled 2.99% and Technology fell 2.01% as Nvidia slid 2.01% on a Stonex Financial downgrade and post-Jensen Huang leadership uncertainty. Kalshi traders price just a 23% chance Nvidia names a permanent CEO before January 2027—led by Rene Haas at 22%, Ian Buck at 18%, and a Huang return at 15%, up 6 percentage points—framing volatility into this week's results alongside liquidity questions around Anthropic's IPO.

Investors rotated into non-AI quality and defensive yield. Consumer Staples led all US sectors with a 1.42% gain ahead of Walmart and Target earnings, Communication Services rose 1.05% as Disney reportedly weighed thousands of job cuts under new CEO Josh D'Amaro, and Financials gained 0.81% as Berkshire Hathaway hit a record high following $9.6 billion in quarterly share buybacks under Greg Abel. In Europe, the STOXX 600 stalled with a 0.02% gain at 664.14 as strategists at Pictet, Bank of America, and State Street warned the region's equity rally is losing momentum.

Sources The Twelfth Magpie, 24/7 Wall St., TradingView, Stocks Down Under, Seeking Alpha, MarketWatch, MarketBeat, Bloomberg.com, Investing.com, Business Post, Kalshi, Yahoo! Finance Canada, Barron's, Yahoo Finance

Asia and Trade: US Tariff Relief for India Contrasts With HK Tech Rout

Hong Kong bore the brunt of US semiconductor selling on Friday, with the Hang Seng dropping 1.55% to 23,637.60 and the Hang Seng Tech Index sinking 3% as Nvidia jitters compounded reports of a fresh US probe into Alibaba's cloud AI chip access. Shanghai slipped 0.59% to 3,634.35 as ING projected Chinese GDP growth will slow to 4.5% in 2027 on lagging consumption, while the Nikkei 225 gained 0.53% to 61,142.35 and Taiwan rose 1.35% to 43,675.12.

Trade diplomacy offered selective relief over the weekend. Indian Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal announced that Indian exports secured preferential US market access with lower tariffs than competitors, pairing with renewed foreign inflows to support Indian equities after the Nifty 50 dipped 0.61% on Friday. Meanwhile, US and Chinese officials held their first Trade Council meeting covering tariffs and farm trade ahead of a planned Trump-Xi summit in Shenzhen.

Sources BusinessToday Malaysia, DD India, Admirals, BBN Times, BigGo Finance, News On AIR, Telegraph India, Investify, Seeking Alpha, TIKR.com, Simply Wall St

Commodities: Yields Sink Silver Below $50 as IEA Sees Oil Surplus

Rising Treasury yields and December Fed rate-hike pricing hammered precious metals despite stubborn inflation expectations. Silver broke below $50 to trade at $49.12 and tumbled nearly 5% on India's MCX, while gold dropped 4.1% on MCX and hovered at $3,886.30 in US futures, just above its $3,856.70 52-week low.

Energy benchmarks faced growing supply headwinds even with Kalshi traders pricing just a 4% chance that Strait of Hormuz shipping normalizes before 2027. The International Energy Agency reported that recovering Gulf exports are set to push the global oil market back into surplus, while Goldman Sachs flagged a global demand hit, capping WTI crude at $88.56 and Brent at $95.84 even as Diamondback Energy reportedly neared a $5 billion Permian acquisition. US natural gas retreated to $3.23 on warmer weather forecasts and an expanding storage surplus.

Sources FXEmpire, WSJ, Reuters, The Times of India, MarketWatch, AP7AM, EnergyNow.com, Kalshi, EnergyNow

Crypto: Relief Bounce From Extreme Fear Faces Monday ETF Reopen

Digital assets stabilized after a weekend selloff wiped out $1 billion in leveraged bullish positions and briefly dragged Bitcoin to a four-month low of $62,080.91 and XRP to $0.89. By Monday morning, Bitcoin rose 0.56% over 24 hours to $65,134.01, Ether climbed 2.32% to $1,826.06, Solana gained 1.88% to $71.91, and XRP rebounded 3.51% to $0.9606, even as the Crypto Fear & Greed Index dropped to 10 on Sunday and real-world asset protocol stUSDT began winding down operations.

Whether the weekend bounce holds hinges on institutional flows when US markets reopen Monday. Spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $2.35 billion over the past two weeks—with BlackRock's IBIT logging an 11-session outflow streak through Friday—and XRP ETFs recorded $19.13 million in weekly redemptions, their largest since March 2026. On Kalshi, traders price a 57% chance Bitcoin holds at or above $64,500 at Friday's US close.

Sources Cointelegraph, BeInCrypto, TradingView, The Defiant, Traders Union, KuCoin, Crypto Briefing, Yahoo Finance, Kalshi
