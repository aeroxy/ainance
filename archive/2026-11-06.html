What happened

Rates steady, volatility spikes

The Federal Reserve held rates steady as inflation and consumer debt remain concerns, while yields moved lower with the 10-year down 5.7bp to 4.835% and the 2-year down 12.1bp to 4.46%. Officials favored a gradual rate-hike path to cool prices if needed, and the dollar index was little changed at 101.59 with USD/JPY down 0.39% to 157.80.

Volatility surged as Wall Street's fear gauge jumped most since Liberation Day, the VIX up 65% to 27.43, as traders eyed payrolls next.

Sources Spectrum News, Reuters, Bloomberg.com, CNBC, WSJ, Yahoo Finance

US tech-led rout unwound the AI trade

US equities reversed sharply with S&P 500 down 4.37%, Nasdaq down 5.55% for its biggest daily drop in 19 months, Dow down 3.47% and Russell down 4.56%. Chips led with semis down 11.51% and tech down 8.05% on AI-regulation and data-center pushback fears, while communications down 0.55%, staples down 0.73% and utilities down 0.66% held up best.

Rotation away from AI stocks to AI landlords was evident, and headlines noted the wipeout was driven by a Democratic sweep raising scrutiny of AI spending and data centers. The selloff was described as the steepest since Liberation Day and the worst post-midterm day since 1896 for chips.

Sources Reuters, Yahoo Finance, Eurasia Business News, LancasterOnline, USA Today, NBC News, Mashable

Asia diverged as Hong Kong rallied on chips rout elsewhere

Asia split: Hang Seng rose 3.67% on heavy volume, with Tencent up 7.17% and Alibaba up 8.45%, while Shanghai was flat up 0.10%. Nikkei fell 1.79% and Taiwan fell 3.20%, and KOSPI selling triggered a trading halt as the chip rout spread.

Singapore's STI rose 0.52% as US midterm uncertainty cleared. In Europe, the STOXX 600 slipped 0.38% on the day but had hit a fresh record as the AI unwind boosted the region while US tech sank.

Sources Investing.com, Finimize, Nikkei Asia, CryptoRank, Moneycontrol.com, myMotherLode.com, The Business Times, Bloomberg.com, Seeking Alpha, WTVB

Crypto slid with tech as regulation overhang grew

Bitcoin fell 4.89% to $76,229 to a seven-week low and Ether fell 7.09% to about $2,249, with Solana down 5.14% as risk-off deepened. Crypto stocks dropped with AI names, Strategy and Coinbase down about 9-10% and Circle down 12%, even as Bitcoin ETF inflows returned and Fidelity inflows offset BlackRock withdrawals.

A Democratic sweep was seen putting crypto on defensive in Washington with US crypto legislation facing delays, adding to deleveraging pressure alongside the equity selloff.

Sources Bloomberg.com, Yahoo Finance, Moomoo, CoinDesk, FXStreet, Crypto News, CNBC, CryptoSlate, 24/7 Wall St.

Oil bounced, gold reclaimed $4,000, metals softened

Oil rebounded from two-month lows with WTI up 3.02% to $90.78 and Brent up 4.14% to $98.54 as traders parsed Iran war outlook, US sanctions on Iran-linked shippers and Ukraine attacks on Russian refineries. Prices remained below $100 despite the bounce, with Citi raising Brent forecasts and flagging $150 if Hormuz is disrupted.

Gold rose 0.87% to $4,108 back above $4,000 for its best day since August and the World Bank sees it holding above $4,000 through 2028. Copper fell 3.39% to a three-month low and natural gas fell 0.44% to $2.95 to an 11-week low on mild weather, record output and above-average inventories.

Sources Crude Oil Prices Today, Bloomberg.com, The Washington Post, WSJ, Barchart.com, Oil & Gas Journal, EnergyNow.com, FXEmpire, 24/7 Wall St., Kitco, Mining.com, Natural Gas Intelligence
