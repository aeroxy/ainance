What happened

Nvidia Leadership Shock Splits AI

Nvidia confirmed CEO Jensen Huang's departure amid reports of board probes into employee relationships, sending its shares down 4.34% to $166.19 on more than triple average volume and prompting analyst downgrades. Governance and guidance shocks rippled across AI infrastructure: Super Micro sank 11.30% on reports of a Justice Department smuggling probe, and Nebius plunged 16.84% after capacity delays forced a cut to its 2026 revenue outlook alongside higher planned capital spending.

Rather than abandoning semiconductors altogether, investors rotated into rival chipmakers, lifting AMD 5.86% and Broadcom 5.54%. That dispersion helped the S&P 500 pare deeper early losses to close down 0.36% at 7,592.26, while the Nasdaq slipped 0.40%. Earlier in Asia, supply-chain jitters hit benchmarks harder, pulling Taiwan's Taiex down 2.63% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng down 1.26% as Alibaba slid 6.76% on reported US plans to curb remote AI chip access.

Sources Yahoo Finance, CNBC, BBN Times, MLQ.ai, Moomoo, Reuters, WSJ, NVIDIA Newsroom

10-Year Tops 5.1% on Hawkish Fed

Treasury yields surged after San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said more rate hikes are needed to cool inflation and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack signaled support for a gradual rate-hike path. The US 10-year yield climbed 13.2 basis points to 5.121%, the 2-year rose 14.8 basis points to 4.875%, and the dollar index gained 0.25% to 103.17.

Rate-sensitive US small caps bore the brunt of the bond selloff, with the Russell 2000 falling 1.55% as the VIX climbed 7.95% to 18.88. Europe moved the other way: the STOXX 600 rose 0.14% to a record 664.01 as upbeat corporate earnings and rotation into non-AI sectors outweighed tech weakness.

Sources WSJ, Reuters, Bloomberg.com, Yahoo Finance, 富途牛牛

Sanctions Lift Oil as Gold Slumps

Crude oil rallied as Middle East diplomatic hopes faded and Western governments tightened pressure on Moscow. Brent jumped 3.63% to $96.13 and WTI rose 2.83% to $88.98 after President Trump dismissed the need for an Iran deal, Britain sanctioned Russian producer Slavneft, and France indicated the EU will approve new Russia sanctions on Monday. Energy was the only US sector to advance, rising 1.51%, while US natural gas gained 3.89% to $3.34 on colder eastern weather forecasts and lower daily output.

Precious and industrial metals slumped under the weight of 5.12% Treasury yields and a firmer dollar. Gold fell 2.91% to a 13-month low of $3,884.10, silver dropped 3.81% to $50.02, and copper slid 1.61% to $6.19.

Sources Kitco, CNBC, BullionVault, Natural Gas Intelligence, EnergyNow.com, Global Banking & Finance Review, Benzinga

ETF Outflows Drag Bitcoin Lower

Cryptocurrencies broke lower alongside risk assets as persistent spot ETF redemptions overwhelmed leveraged dip-buying. Bitcoin fell 4.30% to $71,458.03 with outflows from BlackRock's Bitcoin ETF reaching $2 billion over nine sessions, while Ether dropped 3.12% to $2,017.17 as cumulative Ether ETF outflows neared $700 million.

Altcoins followed Bitcoin south regardless of institutional headlines. Solana dropped 4.30% to $81.38 even after clearinghouse giant DTCC announced plans to integrate Solana for tokenized securities in 2027, and XRP fell 2.75% to $1.04.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Coinpedia, CoinDesk, Cryptonews.net, Crypto News, BigGo Finance
