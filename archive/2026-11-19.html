What happened

Nvidia reversal splits US equities

Nvidia confirmed third-quarter revenue more than doubled from a year ago to a record $115.8 billion, led by $107.6 billion in data-center sales, and guided fourth-quarter revenue above estimates to $130 billion. Yet shares erased a 3% premarket gain to fall 2.89% to $150.96 after Chairman Harvey Jones confirmed the board unanimously accepted Jensen Huang's resignation, rejected reinstating him and formed a national-security compliance committee ahead of his December 1 Congressional testimony. Kalshi traders price a 96% chance Huang testifies before January 2027 and a 4% chance of a permanent CEO before December 1, with Arm chief Rene Haas leading succession odds at 24%.

Elsewhere on Wall Street, growth outpaced cyclicals as investors rotated AI hardware exposure toward Broadcom, which rose 3.65%, and geared up for Anthropic's IPO. The Nasdaq gained 0.40%, the Russell 2000 rose 0.52% and the VIX fell 3.93% to 24.46, lifted by a 1.79% rally in Consumer Discretionary after Target beat earnings estimates and raised guidance. By contrast, the S&P 500 slipped 0.24% and the Dow dropped 1.09% as Energy fell 3.27%, Health Care lost 2.20% on managed-care selling and medtech downgrades, and memory makers SanDisk and Micron fell on lingering NAND glut concerns.

Sources Cointelegraph, Yahoo Finance, Reuters, Investopedia, 24/7 Wall St., Stock Titan, Quantisnow, Kalshi, Stocks Down Under, Benzinga

Hawkish Fed minutes flatten the curve

Minutes from the Federal Reserve's October meeting described holding rates steady as a close call, showing policymakers worried that sticky inflation—including demand and power pressures tied to AI—warranted further tightening in December, with some officials seeing a case for a larger move. Speaking on CNBC, Governor Christopher Waller warned the Fed might need to shock businesses and consumers with a 50-basis-point hike in December after five years of above-target inflation.

Kalshi traders still heavily favor a standard move, pricing a 25-basis-point December hike at 90%, a larger increase at 8% and a hold at 2%. Even so, the hawkish tone pushed the 2-year Treasury yield up 1.1 basis points to 4.800% and lifted the US dollar index 0.29% to a 19-month high of 103.78, while cyclical growth worries pulled the 10-year yield down 3.7 basis points to 5.066%.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, Investor's Business Daily, CNBC, WSJ, Reuters, Barron's, ABA Banking Journal, Kalshi

Metals and crude sink while natural gas breaks out

A stronger dollar and higher-for-longer rate expectations hit metals hard. Copper plunged 5.30% to $5.67 in its steepest one-day drop in over a year as crowded AI data-center and tariff trades unwound alongside weak Chinese demand, pulling Europe's STOXX 600 down 1.03%. Gold fell 3.41% to $3,732.30 and silver tumbled 4.98% to $45.00, sending precious metals to 14-month lows.

Crude oil looked past attacks in the Persian Gulf as physical balances loosened. WTI fell 3.50% to $83.00 and Brent dropped 1.55% to $91.70 after the EIA reported a 3.4 million barrel US crude inventory build, reports detailed a temporary US deal easing Russian oil sanctions to cool prices, and average US retail gasoline fell below $4 per gallon for the first time since July. Natural gas broke away from the energy rout, surging 6.42% to $3.41 past $3.30 resistance on colder US weather forecasts, dipping daily output and record gas-turbine orders.

Sources Benzinga, Transport Topics, Oil & Gas 360, Crude Oil Prices Today, The Michigan Independent, Forbes, BullionVault, FXEmpire, EnergyNow.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, kalkine.ca, BigGo Finance, Mining.com, Barron's, Barchart.com, www.marketscreener.com

Asia diverges on chip curbs and Tencent miss

Hardware-heavy Asian markets caught an early boost from Nvidia's earnings before Wall Street's reversal, with Japan's Nikkei 225 up 0.28%, Taiwan gaining 0.26% and South Korea's KOSPI closing at 5,067.44. Tightened US AI chip export rules and AI spending caution weighed elsewhere: Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.91%, Singapore's STI lost 1.06% and India's Nifty 50 slipped 0.63%.

Pressure on Chinese tech deepened after Hong Kong's close when Tencent posted third-quarter sales below estimates alongside RMB 69.1 billion in AI-related cash outflows, while UBS projected China's GDP growth slowing to 4.5% in 2027. Ahead of APEC talks with US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin criticized protectionism and sanctions, even as India and Thailand advanced bilateral tariff frameworks with Washington.

Sources Cointelegraph, Seeking Alpha, www.tmgm.com, 联合早报, Yahoo Finance Singapore, Bloomberg.com, BigGo Finance, The Edge Singapore, Khaosod English, NEWS10 ABC, News On AIR, Livemint, TradingView

Crypto ETF outflows and CoinShares treasury exit

Bitcoin slid 2.10% to $62,629.87 and BNB fell 1.78% as Fed rate pressure met renewed institutional redemptions. US spot Bitcoin ETFs logged $240 million in net outflows on Tuesday—led by $210 million from BlackRock's IBIT—prompting Citi to trim its 12-month Bitcoin target to $99,532 and CryptoQuant to flag contracting demand, though Glassnode noted resting spot bids outweigh asks 2.3 times near $60,000.

Ether lagged, dropping 2.51% to $1,754.51 after European asset manager CoinShares disclosed liquidating its entire 6,562 ETH treasury for $16.6 million and half its Solana holdings for $2.7 million to fund share buybacks, alongside an eighth day of spot Ether ETF outflows in nine sessions. Binance separately announced a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order from Abu Dhabi courts targeting 26,000 wallet addresses tied to its October hack, though stolen funds already converted into native Bitcoin and Ether cannot be frozen on-chain. Solana held steady, rising 0.04% to $73.11 after its Breakpoint conference and a proposal that could cut account storage deposits sevenfold.

Sources Cointelegraph, Bloomberg.com, Crypto News, BigGo Finance
