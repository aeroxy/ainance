What happened

Anthropic's debut turns into a Wall Street liquidity drain

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 on Wednesday in an $81.3 billion IPO alongside a $26 billion private placement, creating a record $107.3 billion offering. Trading under the ticker ANT, shares opened Thursday at $111.50, up 34.3% for a roughly $2 trillion fully diluted valuation, and briefly reached $115 before tumbling as low as $95.05. Its IPO filing also underscored the capital intensity of the AI build-out, detailing roughly $901 billion in purchase and lease commitments extending to 2045.

Rather than reviving risk appetite, the listing acted as a sell-the-news catalyst across equities. The S&P 500 fell 1.99% to 7,177.76 for a sixth straight loss, the Nasdaq dropped 2.23% to 23,884.59, the Dow sank 2.15%, or 1,084 points, to 49,116.79, and the VIX surged 16.29% to 28.56. Every US sector in our snapshot fell, led by consumer discretionary (-2.76%), communication services (-2.65%), and semiconductors (-2.61%). Nvidia lost 3.30% to $145.98, extending a six-day slide that has erased $611 billion in market value since Jensen Huang's ouster, though optical networking firm Coherent and profit-beating PDD Holdings bucked the rout.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, The New York Times, Eurasia Business News, Moomoo, Barron's, MarketWatch, Yahoo Finance, Quantisnow

Fed minutes and three-hike forecasts cement hawkish repricing

Monetary policy expectations shifted further toward sustained tightening. Minutes from October's Federal Open Market Committee meeting showed officials voted 10 to 2 to hold the benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4%, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting for a quarter-point hike while several other participants called the hold a close call. Participants also warned that AI demand was lifting some consumer prices and flagged rapid, concentrated growth in opaque AI-related debt.

Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson reinforced that view Thursday, saying AI is boosting inflation now while productivity gains remain a future possibility, and insisting inflation return to 2% before any target change. J.P. Morgan forecast hikes at the next three Fed meetings, while Kalshi traders price a 25 bp December hike at 92%, a larger move at 6%, and November CPI above 3.6% year over year at 80%. Equity flight nudged the US 10-year yield down 2.4 bp to 5.042% and the 2-year down 4.0 bp to 4.760%, yet average US 30-year mortgage rates rose to 7.28% and the dollar index edged up 0.02% to 103.88, its highest since April 2025, lifting USD/JPY 0.21% to 159.66.

Sources Cointelegraph, Reuters, CNBC, AP News, American Banker, ABC News, UA.NEWS, Kalshi

Asia splits between AI hardware selling and trade-backed resilience

Regional benchmarks diverged sharply along sector lines. Taiwan tumbled 4.40% to 38,426.89 as TSMC fell 3.45% amid reports of a China chip pause, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.95% to 22,841.62 as Alibaba slid 5.51% on mixed September-quarter results. Europe's STOXX 600 also reversed early AI-led gains to close down 0.88% at 638.41.

Domestic-oriented and trade-beneficiary Asian markets outperformed. India's Nifty 50 climbed 1.14% to 25,514.30 on banking and auto strength as Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said Indian goods will receive preferential US market access with lower tariffs. Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 0.71% to 59,444.21 behind earlier advances in SoftBank and Kioxia, while Singapore's Straits Times Index rose 0.49% to 5,079.49 on a 3.96% gain in Singtel as the Monetary Authority of Singapore detailed a 100 million Singapore dollar, or $77.5 million, tokenized bill settlement using wholesale central bank digital currency.

Sources Cointelegraph, Investor's Business Daily, The Hindu, TradingKey, The Business Times, Yahoo Finance, MLQ.ai, News On AIR, Simply Wall St, Reuters, BeInCrypto

Precious metals break 14-month lows while Hormuz strikes lift oil

Hawkish Fed repricing and a firm dollar stripped precious metals of any equity-rout haven bid. Gold dropped 2.01% to a 14-month low of $3,666.60, silver slid 2.56% to $43.91 for an eighth straight session of losses, and copper fell 0.99% to $5.61. US natural gas sank 3.71% to $3.25 after a larger-than-average storage build outweighed cold-weather expectations.

Crude oil moved the other way as Iran intensified tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, offsetting global surplus projections from the International Energy Agency and a temporary US easing of Russian oil sanctions. WTI crude rose 0.78% to $83.91 and Brent gained 0.64% to $92.54, with Kalshi traders giving only a 3% chance that Hormuz shipping returns to normal before January 2027. US retail gasoline prices have nonetheless posted their steepest three-week drop in nearly two decades ahead of Thanksgiving, sending Kalshi odds of November US gas prices averaging above $3.55 down 18 points to 16%, even as tight diesel and heating oil supplies keep pressure on farmers and Northeast households.

Sources Virginia Business, News Channel 6, Oil & Gas Journal, Bloomberg.com, THV11, The Michigan Independent, FXEmpire, Investing News Network, Forbes Africa, Rediff MoneyWiz, Natural Gas Intelligence, EnergyNow.com, Barchart.com, Kalshi, Cape Cod Times

Bitcoin ETF outflows deepen while tokenized assets advance

Bitcoin fell 0.84% to $62,110.10, ignoring Anthropic's debut even as pre-IPO perpetuals on Binance and Hyperliquid priced within 1% of ANT's $111.50 open. US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $306.4 million Wednesday, cutting net assets to a three-month low of $77.7 billion as outflows hit $2.89 billion in November and topped $2.5 billion for 2026, while long-term holders shed about 121,151 BTC over 30 days. Strategy fell 4.64% to $86.13 on MSCI removal and a drop below its BTC holdings' value, after spending $206.3 million of a $244.7 million stock sale on preferred buybacks instead of BTC. Kalshi traders price $65,000 by Friday afternoon ET at 8% and holding $60,500 at 78%.

Ether rose 0.33% to $1,760.58 and BNB gained 0.37% to $581.26 as six-year-low Binance Ether balances and BNB Chain matching Ethereum in tokenized US Treasury bills offset $40 million in Wednesday spot Ether ETF outflows, a reported $6.3 billion Bitmine loss, and talks that could delay Ethereum's Glamsterdam upgrade to May 2027. XRP fell 0.72% to $0.9619 and Solana dipped 0.08% to $73.06 after issuers shelved income ETFs for both. Democratic SEC nominee Kristin Johnson backed formal rulemaking for tokenized stock trading before the Senate Banking Committee, while Hong Kong's securities regulator added Hyperliquid to its suspicious-platforms alert list.

Sources Cointelegraph, Investor's Business Daily, The Motley Fool, CoinDesk, Crypto News, BigGo Finance, Coinpaper, Kalshi, Crypto Briefing, Yahoo Finance
