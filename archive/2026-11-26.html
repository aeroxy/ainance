What happened

Inflation, Rates and Tariff Timing

October US inflation and growth data reinforced expectations that the Federal Reserve will tighten policy again in December. Bureau of Economic Analysis figures showed headline Personal Consumption Expenditures inflation rose to 3.8% year over year while core PCE accelerated to 3.3%, topping expectations of 3.2%, and third-quarter annualized GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% from 3.4%. Weekly jobless claims fell to 189,000, October new home sales jumped 14.2%, and the Fed's Beige Book pointed to continued expansion alongside sticky prices, leaving traders on Kalshi pricing a 25-basis-point hike at the December 8 to 9 meeting at 92%.

Despite that hawkish data slate and fresh White House Section 301 tariff proposals targeting excess industrial capacity in China, the European Union, and other trading partners, bond yields moved lower as equity volatility spurred safe-haven Treasury buying. The 10-year yield fell 3.8 basis points to 4.864%, the 2-year slipped 1.9 basis points to 4.652%, and the dollar index edged down 0.11% to 103.70, helped after US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC that the proposed 15% to 20% duties would act as a modest top-up and take months to implement.

Sources Cointelegraph, Reuters, WSJ, CNBC, Bloomberg, Kitco, The Washington Post, CNN, Kalshi

US Equities Rotate Out of AI Hardware

Wall Street staged a sharp intraday reversal ahead of Thursday's Thanksgiving closure, paring a morning rout that briefly knocked the Nasdaq down 4.2%. By the close, the S&P 500 was down just 0.28% at 7,181.20 and the Nasdaq lost 0.66% to 23,577.12, while the Dow climbed 0.30% to 50,169.02 and the Russell 2000 rose 0.42%. Materials led sector gains with a 2.07% advance, followed by real estate up 0.92%, communications up 0.89%, industrials up 0.82%, energy up 0.74%, and financials up 0.68%, even as the VIX rose 6.71% to 28.13 on active portfolio hedging.

Semiconductors fell 0.97% and technology lost 0.81% as doubts over peak AI infrastructure margins outweighed strong headline sales. Nvidia slid 3.91% to $149.30 as Monday's rally on Jensen Huang's return faded ahead of his scheduled December Congressional testimony, Micron dropped 4.53% to $601.25 on analyst downgrades and memory demand concerns, and Dell finished up only 0.60% at $297.06 after surrendering an initial post-earnings surge despite doubled sales and a $25 billion buyback. By contrast, traditional PC maker HP Inc. jumped 11.49% on solid earnings, underscoring rotation toward cheaper, cash-generative businesses.

Sources Cointelegraph, Boston Herald, 24/7 Wall St., NAI500, Yahoo Finance, Barron's, CNBC, marketscreener.com, Around Prague, Investor's Business Daily, Kalshi

Asia Tech Sinks Before Wall Street's Recovery

Asian and European markets closed before Wall Street's afternoon rebound and softer US tariff commentary, leaving export-heavy chip hubs sharply lower on Wednesday. Taiwan's Taiex dropped 4.80%, Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.78%, Shanghai lost 2.03%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng declined 1.14%, Singapore's STI slipped 0.57%, and Europe's STOXX 600 shed 1.06%, while media outlets reported South Korea's Kospi plunged 6.2% as Tuesday's semiconductor bounce unwound.

Within Asia, hardware names took heavy single-day hits as Lenovo tumbled 11.07% in Hong Kong and TSMC fell 3.59% in Taipei after Taiwan landed on Washington's tariff radar. India's Nifty 50 bucked the regional slide with a 1.85% gain to 25,819.25, supported by foreign institutional inflows and domestic insulation from manufacturing export tariffs.

Sources Al Jazeera, MLQ.ai, bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com, News On AIR, The Business Times, marketscreener.com, TradingView, CNBC

Oil Jumps on Refinery Strike Claim While Gold Steadies

Energy markets diverged sharply across fuels. Brent crude surged 3.25% to $94.37 and WTI rose 2.41% to $84.03 after Yemen's Houthis claimed an attack on a Saudi refinery and persistent Strait of Hormuz shipping risks offset broader oversupply forecasts. By contrast, US natural gas fell 1.44% to $3.27 despite the season's first storage withdrawal, pressured by ample domestic production and forecasts for milder weather across the US and Europe.

Precious metals stabilized alongside lower Treasury yields. Gold tumbled below $3,500 intraday to a 52-week low of $3,425.20 on hot morning US data before reversing more than 4% off its low to close up 0.25% at $3,570.90, while silver ended virtually flat at $40.77, down 0.02%, and copper rose 0.51% to $5.40.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Oil & Gas 360, Reuters, BullionVault, EnergyNow.com, Investing.com, FXEmpire, Natural Gas Intelligence, Barchart.com, CNBC

Crypto Rebounds Off $57K Despite Record ETF Outflows

Digital assets tracked Wall Street's intraday swings, with total crypto market value rising 0.85% to $2.10 trillion after $512 million in 24-hour liquidations earlier dragged Bitcoin down to $57,209 on Bitstamp. Bitcoin recovered to $60,291.66, up 0.88%, Ether rose 0.86% to $1,690.14, Solana gained 1.95% to $70.64 as Japan's SBI and Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group launched a tokenization venture despite a bug-related halt at Solana DEX Velocity, and XRP climbed 2.46% to $0.9423.

Underlying liquidity metrics remained strained even as spot prices bounced. SoSoValue data showed US spot Bitcoin ETFs hit a monthly record $4.94 billion in November net outflows, Kaiko tracked $12 billion in 30-day stablecoin redemptions concentrated roughly 90% on Ethereum, Galaxy withdrew its bullish Bitcoin call, and miner CleanSpark said it is weighing selling more Bitcoin than it produces after posting a $987 million annual net loss.

Sources Cointelegraph, CoinDesk, Benzinga, Bloomberg, CryptoSlate
