What happened

US steadies after historic AI washout

US equities steadied Friday after a brutal $2.5 trillion AI-led rout. The S&P 500 rose 0.33% to 7,378 and Nasdaq rose 1.09% to 24,580 with the Russell up 1.06%, while the Dow fell 0.46%. Semis led with a 3.63% gain after an 11.5% crash, with memory names up 8 to 10% and Nvidia up more than 6% intraday on a 1-gigawatt data-center retrofitting plan.

The bounce left Nasdaq still on track for a fourth straight weekly decline and VIX at 25.47, down 9.6% but still elevated. Headlines described an anatomy of a selloff with a third-biggest jump in the fear gauge since 1990 and profit-taking on AI bets, but dip buyers offered support into the close.

Strong October payrolls of 165,000 reset rate bets, lifting 10-year yields 2.7bp to 4.864% and 2-year yields 4.3bp to 4.52% while the dollar index rose 0.72% to 102.28. Fed officials pointed to additional hikes and Treasuries stumbled as hike odds rose.

Sources Bloomberg.com, Reuters, WSJ, Times Union, 24/7 Wall St., AP News, Yahoo Finance, Beinsure, TradingView, Stock Titan, Moomoo, MarketWatch

Asia and Europe hit hardest

Asia bore the brunt of contagion. Nikkei plunged 5.96% to 57,434 into a bear market, KOSPI fell 6.02% to 5,059 and triggered a circuit breaker, Taiwan dropped 4.51% in its steepest fall, and Shanghai fell 1.10% with Hang Seng down 1.27% and STI down 2.21%. Europe also slipped with STOXX 600 down 1.00%.

Reports put Asia’s loss above 1.2 trillion dollars as AI regulation fears and a global tech unwind spread after Wall Street’s drop. South Korea halted trading, Japan’s selloff deepened, and risk-off swept European stocks as AI concerns and tariff uncertainty weighed.

Sources Bloomberg.com, Nikkei Asia, BeInCrypto, WION, The Times of India, BigGo Finance, WOWK 13 News, investingLive, Yahoo Finance, Finimize, Sharecast.com, Devdiscourse

Yields up, havens down

Rates and the dollar were the clear winners on the jobs beat. The 10-year and 2-year yield rises alongside a stronger dollar undercut havens. Gold fell 0.95% to 4,074 dollars and silver fell 0.80% to 51.95 dollars, with gold holding just above 4,000 dollars but facing pressure as Goldman cut its year-end forecast on a hawkish Fed shift.

Copper slipped 0.09% to 6.26 dollars and headed for its worst week since March as the AI and tariff trade unwound. Oil consolidated with WTI up 0.27% to 90.76 dollars and Brent down 0.11% to 98.18 dollars, caught between tight global supplies and hopes for Middle East diplomacy. Natural gas rose 0.78% to 2.97 dollars on cool temps and record LNG exports, finishing a topsy-turvy week higher.

Sources WSJ, Bloomberg.com, CNBC, Beinsure, Kitco, Barchart.com, Quartz, Investing.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, Mining.com

Crypto lags on outflows and hack

Crypto slipped with equities. Bitcoin fell 0.45% to 75,882 dollars and Ether fell 0.91% to 2,229 dollars, with Solana little changed at 89.95 dollars and BNB up 0.18%. Outflows dominated, with Bitcoin ETFs seeing about 430 million dollars out Thursday and total crypto ETF outflows topping 540 million dollars.

XRP fell 4.72% to 1.17 dollars, the worst major, hitting an 11-week low after a 10% selloff, while reports noted Binance hackers swapped about 50 million dollars of stolen XRP into Bitcoin. Solana dropped below 100 dollars, prompting dip-buy debates, but ETF holders were described as sitting tight despite losses.

Sources Bloomberg.com, Bitcoin Foundation, Investing.com, CoinGape, Bitcoin Magazine, Yahoo Finance, Investor's Business Daily, CryptoSlate, www.tmgm.com
