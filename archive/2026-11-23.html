What happened

AI Valuation Unwind and Hawkish Fed Odds

US equities logged a seventh straight loss on Friday as the S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73, and the VIX jumped 26.20% to 35.89. Technology fell 7.27% and Semiconductors plunged 10.60%, led by Nvidia's 14.01% slide amid analyst downgrades and post-scandal leadership uncertainty — with Kalshi pricing Rene Haas at 22% and Ian Buck at 14% as next CEO — plus drops of 13.89% in Micron and 13.25% in AMD after a short-seller report alleging China exposure. Anthropic's Nasdaq debut failed to steady AI sentiment, falling 8.50% to $88.91 on Friday and 0.60% after hours ahead of Monday's launch of Defiance 2x Long and 2x Short Anthropic ETFs.

Consumer Staples was the only US sector to advance on Friday, rising 0.67% as Target outperformed rival Walmart on third-quarter results, while Health Care slipped just 0.12%. Flight-to-quality buying pulled the US 10-year Treasury yield down 10.2 basis points to 4.950% and the 2-year yield down 13.0 basis points to 4.649%, even as analysts expect October PCE inflation to hit 3.8% and Kalshi traders price a 25-basis-point December Federal Reserve hike at 90%. Around-the-clock perpetual markets on Binance point to tentative chip stabilization ahead of Monday's US open, with Nvidia, AMD, and Micron perpetuals rebounding 5.55%, 4.75%, and 3.77% from Friday's regular close.

Sources Yahoo Finance, Quantisnow, WSJ, BigGo Finance, TradingView, Kuwait Times, Howl.Link, AFP.com, RealMoney Pro, Investing.com, Moomoo, Kalshi, Barchart.com, CNBC, Calculation

Asian Policy Interventions Meet Global Drag

Asian regulators stepped in over the weekend after regional equities tumbled on Friday, led by a 6.23% drop in Taiwan and a 5.06% slide in Japan's Nikkei 225, alongside a 2.05% decline in Singapore's STI and a 1.89% loss in Hong Kong's Hang Seng. Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission banned short-selling starting Monday following a 7,642-point weekly slide, Chinese authorities cut margin requirements for the first time in 10 months alongside supportive People's Daily commentary, and South Korean officials prepared market-stabilization funds after the KOSPI hit a 10-month low.

Weekend perpetual pricing highlights a timing split between US-traded Asian ETFs and local cash markets. TSMC's Binance perpetual climbed 3.54% from Friday's New York close — a tailwind for US-listed Taiwan and South Korea ETFs after Friday drops of 10.95% and 8.42% — yet remains 3.08% below Friday's Taipei close amid reports TSMC paused 16- and 14-nanometer shipments to Chinese chip designers under US export rules. Elsewhere, Indonesia and the European Union signed the IEU-CEPA pact eliminating tariffs on 90.4% of Indonesian exports by 2027, while China's October new home prices fell again ahead of industrial profit data and Meituan earnings.

Sources Investor's Business Daily, The Standard (HK), BigGo Finance, Seoul Economic Daily, 파이낸셜뉴스, Guangdong News, Business Recorder, JournalArta, Yahoo Finance, Coinpaper, The Economic Times, CNBC, Binance, Calculation

Crypto Weekend Bounce and Bridge Exploits

Digital assets posted a weekend rebound while US spot ETFs were offline, lifting crypto market capitalization 0.82% over 24 hours to $2.08 trillion as Bitcoin rose 0.74% to $60,916.86 and Ether gained 2.04% to $1,721.31, up 4.18% and 7.48% from Friday's US equity close. Thin liquidity on Kraken's Canadian-dollar pair briefly sent Bitcoin to 24,250 Canadian dollars on Saturday — about $16,900, or 71% below its US-dollar price — with Kraken ruling out a breach and upholding trades. Russia also permitted exchange crypto trading from Sunday, while Hong Kong published legislation Friday bringing crypto custodians and advisers under Securities and Futures Commission licensing.

Monday's US reopen pits that weekend bounce against resuming institutional outflows after spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $3.59 billion over three weeks, BlackRock's IBIT logged record daily redemptions, and Kalshi odds of Bitcoin hitting $50,000 before $100,000 climbed 7 percentage points to 30%. Bridge exploits added idiosyncratic pressure: South Korea's Upbit and Bithumb suspended CYBER deposits and withdrawals Sunday after unexpected minting on Solana and an Ethereum bridge outflow sent CYBER down 3.29%, while cross-chain protocol Chainflip confirmed a $12 million vault theft and offered a 10% white-hat bounty.

Sources Cointelegraph, Yahoo Finance, CryptoPotato, kalkine.com, Incrypted, Kalshi, NewsCord, Binance, Calculation

Natural Gas Diverges From Metals and Crude

Energy markets diverged between European gas supply risks and global crude surplus expectations. Natural gas climbed 4.38% on Friday to $3.41 after reports that German utility Uniper received notice from Russia's Gazprom that it cannot meet its gas obligations heading into northern winter demand. WTI crude retreated to $83.41 and Brent settled at $92.57 for a third straight weekly drop, as global surplus forecasts, softer US employment data, and US rig counts rising to 607 outweighed Middle East tensions even as Kalshi odds of more than 10 Strait of Hormuz transits fell 10 points to 84%.

Precious and industrial metals suffered steep weekly breakdowns under hawkish Fed rate expectations. Gold fell 4.62% on Friday to a 52-week low of $3,515.40, snapping its multi-year uptrend, while silver slid 7.41% to $41.07 in a multi-trillion-dollar bullion selloff that nonetheless attracted 6.88-to-1 retail long positioning on Binance's silver perpetual. Copper dropped 2.64% to $5.49 in its worst week of 2026 as US inventories swelled near 2 million tons, prompting traders to reverse course and ship metal back to China despite falling Chinese home prices.
