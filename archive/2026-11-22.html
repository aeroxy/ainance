What happened

US Tech Rout and the Weekend Perpetual Bounce

Friday's AI-led sell-off drove the S&P 500 down 3.70% to 6,910.39, pushing the benchmark into correction territory and below every major Wall Street year-end target according to market commentary, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% to turn negative for 2026 and the VIX jumped 26.20% to 35.89, its highest close since April 2025. Semiconductors plunged 10.60% in what outlets described as their worst week since 2001. Nvidia sank 14.01% to $125.53 amid a reported SEC review and leadership uncertainty following Jensen Huang's resignation, while Anthropic fell 8.50% to $88.91 on its second trading day after testing $83.05, five cents above its $83 IPO price.

Consumer Staples was the only US sector to advance on Friday, rising 0.67%, while non-AI earnings winner Take-Two Interactive gained 0.65% on reported $3.79 billion first-day Grand Theft Auto VI sales. After the bell, oversold tech heavyweights stabilized: Nvidia, Micron, Dell and Alphabet rose 1.77% to 3.49% in Friday extended trade and stood 3.03% to 4.54% above Friday's closes on Binance's 24/7 equity perpetuals on Saturday, alongside gains of 1.96% in S&P 500 and 2.53% in Nasdaq-100 perpetuals. By contrast, newly listed Anthropic slid another 1.25% after hours to $87.80, and downgraded Palo Alto Networks fell 2.72% post-close.

Sources The Washington Post, Yahoo Finance, Reuters, 24/7 Wall St., CEO Economy, MarketBeat, WTOP News, ad-hoc-news.de, Moomoo, Mashable, CNBC, Binance, Calculation

Hawkish Fed Pricing Meets Safe-Haven Bond Buying

Equity turmoil triggered a flight into Treasuries on Friday, pulling the 10-year yield down 10.2 basis points to 4.950% and the 2-year yield down 13.0 basis points to 4.649%, while the yen strengthened as USD/JPY fell 0.72% to 158.53. That bond rally came directly against hawkish monetary policy signals: minutes from the latest Federal Reserve meeting indicated higher rates are needed in December as inflation concerns mount, officials warned that AI investment and tariffs could stoke price pressures, and J.P. Morgan economists forecast three consecutive rate hikes.

Prediction-market traders continue to side with the hawkish policy outlook despite Friday's equity shock. On Kalshi, traders price a 25-basis-point rate hike at the December meeting at 90% and a hike larger than 25 basis points at 2%, while putting the odds of a US recession this year at 4%. Meanwhile, two US House lawmakers introduced legislation on Friday proposing whistleblower awards of 10% to 30% for tips on insider trading in prediction markets, following Thursday's Senate passage of a bill barring contracts tied to war and death.

Sources Cointelegraph, Eagle-Tribune, KATU, www.tmgm.com, Tekedia, CEO Magazyn, Pluang, Kalshi

Asian Tech Slumps While India Draws Foreign Inflows

Semiconductor-heavy Asian bourses bore the brunt of the global tech unwind on Friday, with Taiwan's benchmark dropping 6.23%, South Korea's KOSPI sliding 5.21% below 4,900 to a 10-month low, and Japan's Nikkei 225 falling 5.06%. Taipei-listed TSMC declined 4.76% before New York opened, and its US depositary receipts subsequently tumbled 9.48% amid US chip curbs on China and reports that President Trump threatened sanctions on US tech firms building data centers overseas. European equities also retreated, with the STOXX 600 down 2.00%.

India's Nifty 50 was the only major benchmark in Asia or Europe to post a gain, climbing 1.12% to 25,800.60 near record highs on reported foreign institutional inflows topping 1 trillion rupees. Elsewhere in Asia, Shanghai fell 1.22% and Hong Kong shed 1.89% as calls from China's top economic planner to speed up state-funded projects clashed with skepticism over domestic growth, while the European Union and Indonesia signed a free-trade pact eliminating 90% of tariffs alongside domestic enforcement actions by Indonesia's market regulator.

Sources NAI500, BigGo Finance, 조선일보, The Shillong Times, Bloomberg.com, 联合早报, The Business Times, logos-pres.md, Yahoo Finance, CNBC, Binance

Precious Metals Break Trend as Energy Splits

Rate-hike expectations and fund redemptions battered precious and industrial metals, overriding equity-market volatility. Gold fell 3.98% to $3,539.00 after touching a 52-week low of $3,502.90, breaking its multi-year uptrend as outflows from the GLD and IAU exchange-traded funds accelerated, though Goldman Sachs analysts reiterated a preference for gold over silver on central-bank buying. Silver plunged 7.39% to $41.08, and copper dropped 2.99% to $5.46.

Energy markets diverged sharply by fuel and timeframe. WTI crude rose 1.43% to $83.62 and Brent stood at $92.57 as reported strikes in Iran and falling tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz countered medium-term supply-surplus forecasts. Natural gas futures jumped 6.06% to $3.47 on global supply-disruption headlines even as weak domestic heating demand weighed on US cash prices, while US retail gasoline prices fell to roughly $3.65 a gallon, down 75 cents from this year's peak ahead of Thanksgiving travel.

Sources WSJ, MarketWatch, 24/7 Wall St., investingLive, Benzinga, FOREX.com, Investing.com, EnergyNow.com, Natural Gas Intelligence, Traders Union, CNBC

Crypto Rebounds Off Lows as Weekend ETF Outflows Pause

After sliding with tech stocks on Friday amid $3.47 billion of November US Bitcoin ETF outflows, including a reported record $541 million single-day exit from BlackRock's IBIT, major tokens rallied on Saturday once weekday ETF redemptions paused. Bitcoin climbed 3.52% over 24 hours to $60,469.89 after rebounding nearly 10% from Saturday's $55,130 low as leveraged retail longs were flushed out and perpetual futures traded at a slight discount to spot. Ether rose 5.37% to $1,686.97, XRP gained 5.19% to $0.9338 despite ETF assets slipping below $1 billion, and Solana advanced 4.67% to $70.59 on $4.4 million of spot ETF inflows.

Policy and institutional developments remained mixed across jurisdictions. Reports indicated Russia's sanctioned Moscow Exchange is preparing to launch Bitcoin trading in December, while the European Central Bank invited industry groups to join tokenized-deposit and securities experiments under its Appia project, with initial applications due 11 December. In Hong Kong, authorities published a bill on Friday to bring crypto custodians, advisers and dealers under mandatory Securities and Futures Commission licensing, scheduled for its first legislative reading on 2 December.

Sources Cointelegraph, CoinDesk, CryptoSlate, BeInCrypto, TradingView, KuCoin, Binance
