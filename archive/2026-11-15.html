What happened

Crypto capitulates below $65,000 on sustained ETF outflows

Weekend trading turned a weeklong crypto slide into a forced-liquidation rout. Bitcoin fell 5.94% over 24 hours to $64,812.53, Ether dropped 8.34% to $1,786.16, XRP sank 9.32% below the $1 mark to $0.9280, and Solana tumbled 11.81% to $70.57 as roughly $1 billion in leveraged positions were wiped out and the Crypto Fear and Greed Index fell to 17.

Institutional outflows and internal supply weighed on prices before thin weekend liquidity amplified the break. Farside Investors data cited by Cointelegraph showed US spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $2.35 billion over two weeks, extending BlackRock's IBIT redemption streak to 11 sessions, while XRP ETF outflows hit an eight-month high, the Ethereum Foundation kept selling, and Ethereum layer-2 MegaETH cut 20% of staff. Slower trading is pinching exchanges: Kraken parent Payward posted a 66% year-on-year drop in Q3 adjusted EBITDA to $60 million, though Kalshi traders still price an 84% chance Bitcoin holds $60,000 or higher on November 20.

Sources Cointelegraph, Benzinga, www.trendingtopics.eu, Bitcoin Foundation, BeInCrypto, CryptoRank, Traders Union, Kalshi

AI hardware turmoil drives defensive US equity rotation

US stocks ended Friday split: the S&P 500 fell 0.40% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq lost 0.91% to 25,136.27, while the Dow rose 0.10% to 52,347.55 as major indexes held weekly gains. Semiconductors sank 2.99% and tech slid 2.01%. Nvidia fell 2.01% to $162.85 on an HSBC downgrade ($170 target) and a Wall Street Journal report of an internal probe after CEO Jensen Huang's exit, with Kalshi pricing a 24% chance of a permanent CEO before January 1. AMD sank 8.75%, Broadcom fell 4.76%, Nebius lost 9.92% on a downgrade, CoreWeave shed 6.13% on reported $4 billion loan talks, and weekend reports put Super Micro under DOJ scrutiny over alleged China server smuggling.

Investors rotated steadily into defensive and payment franchises. Consumer staples led all US sectors with a 1.42% advance, energy gained 0.98%, financials rose 0.81%, and health care added 0.54%, helped by analyst upgrades at Walmart and Chevron, while Boston Omaha expanded its buyback authorization to $85 million after the close. Separately, OpenAI rolled out biometric, tokenized AI-agent payments in ChatGPT with Visa, Mastercard, and American Express, and Microsoft confirmed Saturday it resolved a global Microsoft 365 outage caused by a network upgrade rather than a cyberattack.

Sources Cointelegraph, The Motley Fool, MLQ.ai, Business Wire, MarketBeat, 24/7 Wall St., Moomoo, Kalshi, Yahoo Finance, WSJ

Yields top 5.1% as Fed officials flag rate-hike risk

Treasury yields remained a central headwind for long-duration assets as federal debt topped $40 trillion and US inflation exceeded forecasts. The 10-year yield climbed 3.6 basis points on Friday to 5.139%—capping roughly a 30-basis-point weekly surge—while the 2-year yield dipped 1.2 basis points to 4.852% and the dollar index firmed 0.23% to 103.41.

Federal Reserve officials reinforced the hawkish shift: San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said the central bank will likely need to raise rates amid worsening inflation, and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson emphasized that policy cues depend on inflation progress. Prediction-market traders on Kalshi price an 88% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike in December and an 11% chance of a larger move.

Sources CME Group, Reuters, Bloomberg.com, Virginia Business, ABC News, TradingView, Kalshi

Europe hits record as China stimulus pause and Canada rift weigh

Global benchmarks diverged sharply depending on tech and stimulus exposure. Europe's STOXX 600 rose 0.02% to another record at 664.14, wrapping up its strongest week since June as earnings from insurers and oil majors plus UniCredit's $3.5 billion raise offset semiconductor losses. In Asia, Taiwan climbed 1.35% and Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 0.53%, whereas Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.55% to 23,637.60 and Shanghai fell 0.59% after China's central bank halted stimulus pledges and reports of a chip probe hit Alibaba.

Trade policy split along regional lines over the weekend. US-Canada negotiations collapsed after Prime Minister Mark Carney said Washington asked too much and offered too little, with Canada's ambassador warning tariffs are raising business capital costs. Conversely, Reuters reported US and Chinese officials discussed tariffs and farm trade at their inaugural trade council meeting ahead of a planned Xi-Trump summit, lifting soybean futures 1.13% to $1,232.75.

Sources WSJ, Growth Dragons, MLQ.ai, TradingView, Business Recorder, Reuters, The Economic Times, Yahoo Finance, EnergyNow, AgroLatam

Metals and gas retreat as crude weighs Iran sanctions against surplus

Precious and industrial metals retreated under higher yields and dollar strength. Gold settled up 0.06% on Friday at $3,887.60 after losing roughly 5% over the week amid GLD ETF outflows, before Goldman Sachs cut its 2027 gold forecast on Sunday; silver fell 2.83% to $48.65 and copper slid 2.14% to $6.06.

Energy markets balanced geopolitical friction against oversupply. WTI crude rose 0.20% to $88.92—with Brent at $95.84—as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent vowed the toughest-ever sanctions on Iran and President Trump questioned the need for an Iran deal, countering an International Energy Agency assessment that global oil supply has returned to surplus. Heating oil added 0.47% to $4.31, remaining up 59% year on year, while US natural gas fell 2.93% to $3.24 on record production, a larger-than-usual storage build, and strong El Nino winter forecasts.

Sources WSJ, CNBC, The Guardian, Benzinga, Investing.com, The New Indian Express, EnergyNow.com, Finimize
